# The Puzzle-Box Job

## One-Shot Campaign Brainstorming

# Session Overview

Our players are **skilled thieves-for-hire**, contracted by an **anonymous employer** to steal a **mysterious puzzle box** from the estate of **Lord Veymar**.

They have **no idea what's inside** or **why it's so important**, only that they must **deliver it unopened before the night is over**.

This session is a **player-driven heist**, balancing:
- **Stealth** – Avoiding detection in a dangerous environment.
- **Social Deception** – Bluffing past guards and mingling with nobles.
- **Action** – When things go wrong, fighting or fleeing.
- **Problem-Solving** – Overcoming obstacles and moral dilemmas.

The one-shot is structured into **six acts**, each with **multiple paths, branching choices, and consequences that ripple through the session**.

## The Six-Act Structure

Each act represents **a critical phase of the heist**. Players have freedom to **approach each phase differently**, shaping the story through their actions.

| Act | Phase of the Heist | What Happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Job Offer | The players meet their mysterious employer and accept the mission. |
| 2 | Gathering Intel & Planning | They collect information to prepare for the heist, choosing how much risk to take. |
| 3 | Infiltration: Getting Inside | They choose how to break into the estate, dealing with guards and obstacles. |
| 4 | Navigating the Mansion | They must stealthily or socially move through the mansion toward the puzzle box. |
| 5 | Retrieving the Puzzle Box | Finding and securing the prize is harder than they thought—mysteries and dangers emerge. |
| 6 | The Escape & The Fallout | The heist's conclusion—do they get out clean, or does everything fall apart? |

Every act contains **six different ways to handle the situation**, giving players options while keeping the GM prepared for any approach.

## Prelude: The Job Offer

The session begins in a **smoky tavern, a candlelit study, or a shadowy back alley**, where the players meet **Locke**, the middleman for their employer.

> "The job is simple—get in, steal the puzzle box, get out. No questions. No delays. And whatever you do... do not open it."

**Key Details the Players Learn:**
- **Target:** Lord Veymar's heavily guarded estate.
- **Objective:** Steal a **puzzle box** from his study.
- **Employer:** Anonymous. Pays well, but keeps secrets.
- **Conditions:** The box **must not be opened.**

**GM Prep:**
- Introduce Locke's **mysterious nature** and give the players a chance to ask questions.
- Allow them to **negotiate payment, demand extra intel, or push for more details.**
- If they **decline the job**, introduce **a rival crew** who will take it instead—raising the stakes.

Once they accept, the heist begins.

## Act 1: Gathering Intel & Planning

Before breaking into the estate, players can **gather information** to improve their odds. Every method has **risks and potential consequences**.

| Intel-Gathering Method | What They Might Learn | Possible Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scouting the Mansion | Guard patrols, hidden entrances | Getting spotted raises security |
| Bribing a Servant or Guard | Layout details, vault location | They might betray the players |
| Speaking to Veymar's Family/Rivals | Secrets, blackmail material | Getting entangled in noble politics |
| Checking the Black Market | Underground rumors, rival thieves | Word might spread, drawing attention |
| Drinking with the Guards | Schedules, security flaws | Risk of saying too much or getting drunk |
| Gossiping with the Groundskeepers | Hidden paths, old tunnels | Some servants are still loyal to Veymar |

**GM Prep:**
- **Players don't have to gather intel,** but if they do, let it meaningfully impact the heist.
- If they fail an intel roll, **consider adding a complication later.**

## Act 2: Infiltration – Getting Inside

How do the players **enter the estate**? Each method has **its own risks and rewards.**

| Entry Method | Approach Type | Best for Players Who... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Main Gate | Bluff or Force | Prefer social deception or combat. |
| 2. Servant's Entrance | Stealth or Bribery | Favor sneaky infiltration. |
| 3. Scaling the Wall | Climbing & Acrobatics | Like agility-based entry. |
| 4. The Sewers | Stealth & Survival | Prefer unconventional routes. |
| 5. Hidden Garden Passage | Stealth & Animal Handling | Like distraction tactics. |
| 6. Fake Invitation | Social Manipulation | Enjoy high-stakes bluffing. |

## Act 3: Navigating the Mansion

Once inside, they must **reach the study** without getting caught.

| Path Taken | Description | Challenges |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The Grand Hallway | Social deception route | Nobles & guards watching for intruders |
| 2. Servant's Quarters | Hidden corridors | Nosy staff, locked doors |
| 3. Rooftops & Balconies | Stealth route | Slippery footing, patrolling archers |
| 4. Hidden Passageways | Forgotten tunnels | Traps, ghosts of the past |
| 5. Ballroom & Galleries | Intrigue & social drama | Gossiping nobles, watchful stewards |
| 6. Hedge Maze & Conservatory | Outdoor stealth | Guard dogs, eerie plant life |

## Act 4: Retrieving the Puzzle Box

The **puzzle box is locked inside a secure room.** How do they get it?

| Method | Description |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Arcane Vault | A magical lock puzzle must be solved. |
| 2. Hidden Bookshelf | A riddle reveals a secret compartment. |
| 3. Reinforced Safe | Strength-based brute force. |
| 4. Glass Display Case | Dexterity & trap-disarming. |
| 5. Desk Drawer with Key | Requires prior key theft or bribery. |
| 6. Veymar Has It | Must steal it off him directly. |

## Act 5: The Escape & The Fallout

With the box in hand, **things go wrong.** What happens?

| Escape Challenge | Danger Introduced |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Arcane Lockdown | Magical defenses activate. |
| 2. The Burning Library | A failsafe ignites the room. |
| 3. Hunted by Veymar | He chases them himself. |
| 4. The Rival Crew | Another team intercepts them. |
| 5. The Curse Activates | The box is more than it seems. |
| 6. The Employer Betrays Them | They were never meant to succeed. |

The **final choice**: **Do they deliver the box—or open it?**

## Act 6: The Final Act

> "You got what you came for. Now what?"

The players **escaped with the puzzle box**—but the job isn't truly over. This final act is about **choices and consequences**. Now that they **hold the prize**, they must decide what to do next.

Each ending is based on **key choices made throughout the heist**—how they **stole the box, how they escaped, and what they learn about their employer.**

### Overview: The Six Possible Endings

| Final Act Title | What Happens? | Key Themes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The Betrayal at the Drop-Off | The employer turns on them—the job was never meant to be clean. | Trust, deception, survival. |
| 2. The Truth Inside the Box | The players open the puzzle box, revealing a horrifying or miraculous secret. | Curiosity, consequences, cosmic horror. |
| 3. Hunted by Lord Veymar | Veymar was prepared for this—and now he is hunting them down. | Pursuit, paranoia, unfinished business. |
| 4. The Rival Thieves' Ambush | Another faction wants the box—and they've been watching from the start. | Greed, competition, power struggle. |
| 5. The Curse Takes Hold | The box is not just an object—it's an event, and the players are now part of something much bigger. | Supernatural consequences, eldritch horror, fate. |
| 6. The Perfect Crime (Or Was It?) | The heist was a complete success, but now the players must decide what to do next. | Victory, greed vs. morality, hidden dangers. |

### The Story Ends, But...

No matter the choice, **one thing is certain:**

**The heist is over.** But was it ever really just a heist?
