# The Puzzle-Box Job — DM Guide

> **System:** D&D 5e | **Level:** 6 | **Players:** 5 | **Runtime:** 2-3 hours
> **Setting:** Waterdeep — Dock District (base) / Sea District (heist)

---

## Quick Reference

### Heat Tracker

Track how much attention the party draws throughout the heist. Start at **Level 0**.

| Heat Level | Status | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Unnoticed** | Everything is going according to plan. No extra obstacles. |
| 1 | **Suspicious** | Guards are alert. +2 to all guard Perception checks. Random patrols appear. |
| 2 | **Alarmed** | The estate is on high alert. Doors are locked. Arcane Lockdown triggers during escape. |

**What raises Heat:**
- Failing a Stealth or Deception check by 5+ → +1 Heat
- Knocking out or killing a guard whose absence is noticed → +1 Heat
- Triggering a trap or alarm → +1 Heat (instant to Level 2 if magical alarm)
- Failed intel gathering that tipped someone off (from Act 1) → +1 Heat at start of infiltration

**What lowers Heat:**
- Clever distraction that draws guards away (player creativity) → -1 Heat
- Disguising evidence of their passage (hiding a body, cleaning up) → -1 Heat

- **Deception is terrible** — best is Jean at +3. They cannot lie their way through encounters. They'll lean on Performance, Persuasion, Intimidation, and stealth instead

## Act 1 — The Job Offer (~15 min)

**Before you start:** Martin Storm's character sheet shows 0 gold, 0 items, and no armor (AC 12 unarmored). Confirm with Robben before the session whether this is intentional or an oversight. If an oversight, provide standard Bard starting equipment (leather armor, rapier, etc.).

### Setting the Scene

> **Read Aloud:**
> *The Rusty Anchor sits at the edge of the Dock District where the salt air meets the stink of low tide. You've been here before — it's the kind of place where nobody asks questions and everybody minds their own business. Tonight, a corner booth has been reserved. A single candle burns on the table. The man sitting there watches you approach with the calm patience of someone who has never been surprised in his life.*
>
> *"Sit," he says. "We have business."*

### Locke's Briefing

> *Locke slides a folded piece of parchment across the table — a rough sketch of a manor estate — and taps it once with a gloved finger.*
>
> *"Lord Aldric Veymar. Sea District. You know the type — old money, high walls, the kind of man who collects things he doesn't understand. His estate is a three-story manor behind stone walls and iron gates. Tonight, he's hosting a gala. Half the nobility of Waterdeep will be there, which means the place will be full of people, noise, and distractions. Perfect cover."*
>
> *He leans back, fingers interlalced.*
>
> *"On the third floor, in his private study, there is a puzzle box. Small — fits in the palm of your hand. That is your objective. Get inside, take the box, get out. Simple work for people of your talents."*
>
> *He reaches into his coat and sets a leather pouch on the table. It clinks heavily.*
>
> *"Five hundred gold pieces each. Half now, half when you deliver the box to me before dawn. Do you find these terms, agreeable?"*
>
> *(DC 18 Persuasion to negotiate up to 750 gp each. Martin's +5 and Jean's +6 make this achievable together via Help action. Bardic Inspiration makes it very likely.)*

> *His expression shifts. The smile stays, but the warmth drains from it.*
>
> *"One condition. One rule. Non-negotiable."*
>
> *He leans forward.*
>
> *"Do not open the box. I cannot stress this enough. You will not be paid if the seal is broken. You will not be forgiven. Deliver it sealed, and we part as friends. Break it open, and... well, let's just say I can be less friendly."*

**[Pause here.]** Give Lómë an **unprompted Religion DC 12 check** (she has +6, needs 6+, very likely to succeed). On success, read to her:

> *Something about how Locke speaks about the box — the gravity, the specificity — reminds you of the warnings in Kelemvor's scripture about sealed artifacts. Containers of souls. Instruments of final death. You've read about things like this. They're not meant to be moved.*

Then continue:

> *"Do we have an agreement?"*

### Foreshadowing (Subtle)

Plant these details naturally during the conversation — don't force all of them:

**If players ask why he needs exactly five people:**
> *Locke tilts his head, as if the question amuses him. "Five is the right number for a job like this. Fewer draws suspicion. More invites chaos. Five is... elegant."*
>
> *(The real reason: five sacrifices.)*

**Locke's gloves:** He wears fine leather gloves and never removes them, even when handling the gold. If someone comments or asks:
> *"An old habit. I have... sensitive hands."*
>
> *(Rakshasa hands are inverted — palms where the backs should be.)*

**If anyone rolls Insight (DC 20):** *(Lómë has the best shot at +6 — needs 14. Martin gets +4 from Jack of All Trades — needs 16. Jean has -1; he'll notice nothing.)*
> *Something about Locke feels rehearsed. His mannerisms are perfect — too perfect. He moves like a man, speaks like a man, but there's a quality to it, like watching someone who has studied what a person is supposed to look like and is performing the role flawlessly. Almost.*

**If anyone offers Locke food or drink:**
> *He raises a gloved hand. "No, thank you. I've already eaten." His smile doesn't waver, but for a moment, something flickers behind his eyes — a private joke.*

### If Players Decline

> *Locke's expression doesn't change. He reaches for the leather pouch and begins pulling it back across the table.*
>
> *"A shame. Truly. I had hoped your reputation was earned." He pauses, then adds, almost casually: "I suppose I'll send word to the Jackdaws. Ren's crew. You know them, I'm sure — loud, sloppy, but willing. They've been asking about this job for a week."*
>
> *He stands, straightening his coat.*
>
> *"Of course, if Ren's people get to the box first, any future opportunity I might have brought your way goes with them. But that's your choice."*

This should trigger competitive pride. If they still refuse, the one-shot pivots to them racing the Jackdaws for the box (improvise).

### Transition

> *Locke pushes the sketch of the estate toward you and stands, pulling on his coat.*
>
> *"The gala begins at sundown. That gives you a few hours to prepare — scout the grounds, talk to the right people, do whatever it is you do. I don't care how you get in. I care that the box is in my hands before dawn."*
>
> *He sets a second, smaller piece of parchment on the table — an address in the Dock District.*
>
> *"When you have it, come here. I'll be waiting."*
>
> *He turns to leave, then pauses.*
>
> *"And remember — do not open the box."*
>
> *He walks out without looking back.*

## Act 2 — Gathering Intel & Planning (~20-30 min)

### After Locke Leaves

> **Read Aloud:**
> *The door to the Rusty Anchor swings shut behind Locke. The noise of the tavern rushes back in — the clink of mugs, the murmur of drunken conversation, the creak of floorboards. On the table in front of you: a rough sketch of an estate, a Dock District address on a scrap of parchment, and a pouch of gold that's heavier than it has any right to be.*
>
> *Outside, the afternoon sun is already starting its descent. You have maybe three, four hours before sundown. Before the gala. Before this thing becomes real.*
>
> *So — what's the plan?*

Let the players talk. Give them a beat. If they jump into planning immediately — great, follow their lead. But if they hesitate or look to you for direction, use the prompts below.

### If Players Ask: "What Do We Already Know?"

This is what their characters would reasonably know as experienced locals in the Dock District:

> *"Here's what you know. Lord Aldric Veymar is old Waterdeep money — Sea District nobility, the kind of family that's had a seat at the table since before your grandparents were born. His estate is one of the bigger ones on the ward's north side — three stories, stone walls, iron fence, private grounds. You've seen it from the outside. Everyone has. It's not the kind of place you just walk into."*
>
> *"Veymar himself is known as a collector — rare books, arcane artifacts, things pulled from ruins and shipwrecks. He's eccentric, private, and paranoid. Not well-liked among the other nobles, but too rich to ignore."*
>
> *"Tonight, he's hosting one of his galas. These are invitation-only affairs — wealthy merchants, minor nobility, the occasional visiting dignitary. Heavy security. But also a lot of people, a lot of noise, and a lot of distractions."*
>
> *"You don't know the layout inside the mansion. You don't know what security looks like beyond the front gate. You don't know where the study is or what's protecting the box. But you know this district, you know these streets, and you have a few hours to figure the rest out."*

### If Players Ask: "What Can We Do?" or Seem Stuck

Don't read a list. Instead, frame it as their characters thinking through their options:

> *"Alright — you've got a few hours and a target you know almost nothing about. Think about what you'd normally do before a job like this."*
>
> *"You could head over to the Sea District and scope the place out yourself — see the walls, the gates, the guard patrols. Get eyes on it before tonight."*
>
> *"Veymar's got household staff — servants, kitchen workers, groundskeepers. People like that are usually underpaid and easy to talk to, if you know how to ask."*
>
> *"A man like Veymar has rivals. Other nobles who'd love to see him embarrassed. The right conversation at the right tea house could turn up useful dirt."*
>
> *"This is the Dock District. Somebody here knows something. Knuckles down at the market always has his ear to the ground — and he usually has useful things for sale, too."*
>
> *"Some of Veymar's estate guards drink at the taverns around here when they're off duty. A few rounds of ale can loosen a lot of tongues."*
>
> *"Or there's the grounds crew — gardeners, stable hands. People who work the outside of the estate and know every path, every door, every shortcut."*

**DM Note:** You don't have to offer all six. Read the room — offer 2-3 that match your players' style. If nobody bites, just pick the one you think will be most fun and have an NPC approach *them* (e.g., Pip bumps into them on the street, looking nervous). 

**Oda will almost certainly want to Wild Shape scout** — see note under Option 1. If she does, count it as one of their 3 intel attempts and steer the remaining 2 toward social options (Pip, Thorne, Knuckles, guards) that Wild Shape can't replace.

### Pacing Rule

Players can pursue up to **3 intel attempts** before the gala begins. After 3 attempts (or whenever the energy starts to flag), trigger the **Transition to Act 3** at the end of this section.

### The Six Intel Options

**1. Scouting the Mansion** *(Best sent: Oda via Wild Shape, or the Rogue with Stealth expertise)*

**If Oda Wild Shapes to scout** (spider, rat, cat, or bird — CR 2, lasts 3 hours): She can learn guard rotation patterns, the servant's entrance, layout of the grounds, the conservatory/hedge maze, and exterior entry points. She **can't** learn insider knowledge (Pip's dumbwaiter intel), political gossip (Thorne's ward warning), or street-level intel (Knuckles' items for sale) — Wild Shape provides eyes, not conversations. 

**Countermeasures:** the Mastiffs react to wild-shaped animals with hostility (keen smell, Perception +3); the arcane ward on the study door pulses when a shapechanger approaches within 10 ft (warning tingle in beast form); estate staff kill vermin on sight; bird form gives an aerial view but can't enter the building. If she succeeds, reward the creativity — grant the scouting intel for free and move on to social options.

> *The walk from the Dock District to the Sea District takes about twenty minutes, and the difference hits you before you even cross the ward line. The streets get wider. The buildings get taller. The smell of fish and tar gives way to clean stone and flowering window boxes.*
>
> *The Veymar estate sits on the north end of the ward — you spot the pale stone walls and iron fence from two blocks away. Even from a distance, you can see activity: servants carrying crates of wine, a florist's cart unloading arrangements, the first carriages beginning to arrive for tonight's gala. Two guards in polished half-plate stand at the main gate, watching the street with professional disinterest.*
>
> *There's a low stone wall across the street with a good sightline. If you're careful, you could watch the place for a while without being noticed.*

- **Check:** Stealth DC 14 (to observe without being seen) + Perception DC 12 (to notice useful details)
- **Success (Stealth pass, Perception fail):**

> *You find a spot across the street — a low stone wall with a decent sightline — and settle in. For the better part of an hour, you watch. Servants come and go. Carriages pull up, deposit well-dressed guests, and rattle off again. The two guards at the main gate rotate with a second pair at some point, but honestly, from this distance, it's hard to tell much else.*
>
> *The estate is big. The walls are high. There are definitely guards. Beyond that? You'd need to get closer — or find someone who's actually been inside.*

**What they learn:** The estate is well-staffed and busy with gala prep. Two guards on the main gate at all times. That's it — no patrol routes, no hidden entrances, no useful details. They confirmed the job is real, but didn't gain a meaningful advantage.

- **Success (Stealth pass, Perception pass):**

> *You find a spot across the street — a low stone wall with a decent sightline — and settle in. For the better part of an hour, you watch. And you start to notice things.*
>
> *The two guards at the main gate rotate every thirty minutes — a second pair comes from inside, and they swap without overlap. For about two minutes during each change, only one guard is actually watching the street while the other walks back in. That's a window.*
>
> *On the east side of the estate, there's a smaller door — a servants' entrance. Kitchen staff use it to haul in deliveries. It's not guarded, but someone inside checks it every few minutes. You watch a boy in a stained apron prop it open with a crate while he has a smoke. He doesn't close it when he goes back in.*
>
> *And one more thing — there's a section of the north wall, near the back gardens, where the iron fence meets an old stone wall. Ivy has grown thick over it. The top of the wall is lower there — maybe eight feet. No one patrols that section. At least, not while you were watching.*

**What they learn:** Guard rotation every 30 min with a 2-minute gap at the main gate. Unguarded servants' entrance on the east side (often propped open). A climbable section of the north wall near the gardens (~8 ft, ivy-covered, unpatrolled). This is the best possible scouting result — it opens up multiple infiltration advantages.

- **Failure (Stealth):** A guard spots them lingering. +1 Heat at the start of infiltration. The guard will remember their face.

**2. Bribing a Servant (Pip)**

> *You find what you're looking for at a cheap bakery near the ward line — the kind of place estate servants go on their breaks because they can't afford to eat where they work. A skinny young man in a stained kitchen apron is sitting alone at a corner table, picking at a meat pie and looking like he'd rather be anywhere else. He's got the look — underpaid, overworked, and one bad day from quitting.*

- **Check:** Investigation DC 12 (to find a disgruntled servant) + Persuasion DC 13 or 50 gp bribe (no check needed with gold). *(Martin +5 or Jean +6 — easy for either, needs 8+ or 7+.)*
- **Success:**

> *Pip glances over both shoulders, then leans in close. He smells like onion soup and anxiety.*
>
> *"The study — it's third floor. Big oak door, always locked. Lord Veymar keeps the key on him, on a chain around his neck, never takes it off. I've never been up there — nobody has except him and the Captain."*
>
> *He chews his lip.*
>
> *"But there's — look, there's an old dumbwaiter in the kitchen. Service lift, yeah? Goes up to the second floor, to the hallway outside the guest rooms. Nobody uses it anymore, but it still works. I've seen the pulleys move. If you're small enough — or clever enough — that's a way up without being seen."*
>
> *He holds out his hand, palm up.*
>
> *"We never spoke. Yeah?"*

- **Failure (Persuasion):** Pip gets scared and reports them. +1 Heat at infiltration. Pip won't talk again.

**3. Speaking to Veymar's Rivals**

> *It doesn't take long to find someone who dislikes Lord Veymar — the harder part is finding someone willing to talk about it openly. A few well-placed questions at a Sea District tea house lead you to Lord Cassius Thorne, a silver-haired nobleman with sharp features and the comfortable posture of a man who has never worried about money. He's holding court at a corner table, half-finished glass of wine in hand, and he raises an eyebrow when Veymar's name comes up.*

- **Check:** Investigation DC 14 (to find the right noble contact) + Persuasion or Intimidation DC 15. *(Jean is a natural — Knight background, "eloquent flattery" personality, Persuasion +6 vs DC 15 needs 9+. Martin can assist with Performance +7.)*
- **Success:**

> *Lord Cassius Thorne swirls his wine and examines you with the amused detachment of someone who enjoys watching other people's problems.*
>
> *"Aldric Veymar. Oh, where to begin. Three months ago, he purchased the salvage rights to a shipwreck off the Sword Coast — paid a fortune for it, outbid half the Merchant's Guild. Everyone assumed it was about the cargo. Silks, spices, the usual. But no."*
>
> *He leans in, lowering his voice to a conspiratorial hush.*
>
> *"He found something on that ship. Some kind of box. Small thing — you could hold it in one hand. And ever since, he's been... different. Obsessed. He cancelled his standing appointments at the club. Dismissed half his household staff. Spends every evening in his study with the door locked."*
>
> *He taps the rim of his glass.*
>
> *"Speaking of which — if you're planning to visit that study, you should know he's had it warded. Magical protections on the door. Quite serious ones, from what I hear. The kind you hire a wizard for."*
>
> *He smiles thinly.*
>
> *"I do hope whatever you're planning causes him enormous inconvenience."*

- **Arcane ward:** DC 16 Arcana or Thieves' Tools to bypass.
- **Failure:** Thorne reports their inquiries to Veymar. +1 Heat.

**4. Checking the Black Market (Gareth "Knuckles")**

> *Gareth "Knuckles" Brune operates out of a stall in the Dock District's undermarket — a maze of canvas-covered tables and unmarked crates that technically doesn't exist, as far as the City Watch is concerned. You find him where he always is: leaning against the back wall behind a counter of "salvaged goods," a heavyset man with scarred knuckles and eyes that have already appraised you, your gear, and your coin purse before you've said a word.*

- **Check:** Streetwise/Investigation DC 12 (Dock District characters have advantage). *(The Rogue fits thematically. The Masterwork Thieves' Tools +2 bonus are valuable for Act 5 — encourage purchase if the Rogue's base tools aren't enough.)*
- **Success:**

> *Knuckles leans against the back wall of his stall, arms folded, chewing something that might be tobacco and might not be.*
>
> *"Veymar's box. Yeah, I've heard the whispers. You're not the first crew asking about it — Ren's Jackdaws have been poking around all week. Sloppy about it, too. If they haven't tipped off Veymar's people already, they will."*
>
> *He spits.*
>
> *"Word of advice? Move fast. Move tonight. And if you need an edge —"*
>
> *He reaches under the counter and produces two items: a small crystal vial of shimmering liquid and a leather roll of gleaming steel tools.*
>
> *"Potion of Invisibility. Three hundred gold. Or masterwork picks — finest set in the Dock District. Two hundred. Take one, take both, take neither. I don't give refunds and I don't remember faces."*

- **Potion of Invisibility** (300 gp) or **Masterwork Thieves' Tools** (+2 bonus, 200 gp).
- **Failure:** Word gets out. +1 Heat.

**5. Drinking with the Guards**

> *The Broken Oar is two blocks from the Rusty Anchor and caters to a rougher crowd — off-duty soldiers, dock workers, and estate guards who want to drink somewhere their employers won't see them. A couple of men in plain clothes are sitting at the bar, but you clock them immediately — the posture, the calluses, the way they scan the room out of habit. These are Veymar's guards, off shift and three ales into forgetting about it.*

- **Check:** Constitution DC 13 (to stay sober) + Deception or Persuasion DC 14 (to steer conversation). *(Send Jean — Knight background, "insatiable desire for decadent pleasures" is his Flaw, CON +2 needs 11+, Persuasion +6 needs 8+. Great RP opportunity.)*
- **Success:**

> *The guard — a stocky man with a crooked nose and three ales deep — slings an arm around the bar and grins.*
>
> *"Helm? Oh, she runs a tight ship, don't get me wrong. But even the Captain's got her routine. Every night at midnight — midnight sharp, mind you — she goes up to report to Lord Veymar in his study. Twenty minutes, like clockwork. That's when things get... relaxed on the grounds, if you take my meaning."*
>
> *His buddy chimes in, sloshing his drink:*
>
> *"And the ballroom — the east balcony? Nobody watches it during the performances. Everyone's inside watching the dancers. You could walk a horse across that balcony and nobody'd notice."*
>
> *The first guard laughs and claps you on the back.*
>
> *"Why d'you want to know all this, anyway? You're not thinking of robbing the place, are you?"*
>
> *He laughs like it's the funniest joke he's ever told.*

- **Failure (Constitution):** The PC gets drunk. Disadvantage on the first ability check of the infiltration. The guards laugh it off — no Heat.
- **Failure (Deception):** The guards get suspicious. +1 Heat.

**6. Gossiping with Groundskeepers**

> *The grounds crew doesn't work inside the estate — they work around it, and that means you can find them outside the walls. An old man in a wide-brimmed hat is trimming hedges along the public side of the fence, working with the slow, methodical pace of someone who's been doing this exact job for longer than you've been alive. He's got dirt on every surface of his body and a watering can that looks older than the estate itself.*

- **Check:** Persuasion DC 12 or Animal Handling DC 14 (helping with the grounds earns trust). *(Oda — Outlander background, Animal Handling +6 needs 8+. Also sets up her ability to calm the Mastiffs later at DC 15.)*
- **Success:**

> *The old gardener — weathered hands, dirt under every nail — straightens up from his work and squints at you.*
>
> *"Been tending these grounds thirty years. Thirty years. I was here when Lord Veymar's father built the conservatory. And his father before that, he built the tunnels."*
>
> *He gestures vaguely toward the glass-walled building at the edge of the gardens.*
>
> *"There's a passage under the conservatory floor. Stone trapdoor, behind the big fern — the one with the red leaves. Goes down to the wine cellar. The old lord used it to move cases without tracking mud through the house. Nobody uses it anymore. Nobody remembers it."*
>
> *He pauses, then adds:*
>
> *"Oh — and stay out of the hedge maze after dark. Two mastiffs patrol it. Big ones. Veymar's idea. Mean as sin unless you know how to talk to dogs."*

- **Failure:** A loyal servant informs the household. +1 Heat.

### GM Notes
- Track which intel they gathered — it opens or closes options in later acts.
- If they got the tunnel info, they can use it in Act 3.
- If they learned about the arcane ward, they can prepare to bypass it in Act 5.
- If they got nothing? That's fine. The heist is harder but still possible.

### Transition to Act 3 — Sundown

After the players have completed their intel attempts (or whenever the energy starts to flag), read this:

> **Read Aloud:**
> *The sun touches the horizon, and the Sea District begins to glow. Lanterns flicker to life along the ward's main streets. From the direction of the Veymar estate, you can hear the faint sound of music starting up — strings, something elegant, the kind of music that costs more than your rent.*
>
> *The gala has begun. Carriages are rolling through the streets. The gates are open. The house is full of noise, light, and a hundred potential witnesses.*
>
> *This is it. Whatever you've learned, whatever you've planned — it's time to put it to work.*
>
> *How are you getting in?*

**DM Note:** If the players haven't discussed their entry plan yet, give them a minute to strategize. If they need a push, remind them of what they learned: *"You know about the servants' entrance on the east side..."* or *"Knuckles mentioned the Jackdaws are also moving tonight — you may not want to wait."* Then move to Act 3.

## Act 3 — Infiltration: Getting Inside (~20-30 min)

The gala is underway. Carriages line the circular drive. Music and light pour from the ballroom windows. The estate is alive with activity — perfect cover, or a den of watchful eyes.

> **Read Aloud:**
> *The Veymar estate rises from the Sea District like a monument to old money. Three stories of pale stone, ivy-laced walls, and tall arched windows glowing amber from within. A wrought-iron fence rings the property, its gate flanked by two guards in polished half-plate. Beyond, you can hear the murmur of a hundred conversations and the lilting notes of a string quartet.*

This party will almost certainly split. The natural breakdown: **Martin + Jean** as the social team (Martin's Entertainer background + Performance +7, Jean's Knight background + Persuasion +6 — avoid the Fake Invitation route, DC 16 Deception is brutal for them at +1 and +3). **Rogue + Oda** as the stealth team (Wild Shape + lockpicking, aim for the study). **Lómë** is the wildcard — she can join the social group as Jean's "spiritual advisor" (Knight households often have chaplains), take the sewer route (CON +2 handles DC 12), or disguise as a servant ("new temple hire" for the household chapel).

### Entry Methods

The party should choose **one approach** (though creative combinations are fine). Each entry has a primary check and a consequence for failure.

**1. Main Gate — Bluff or Force**

> *The main gate is a grand affair — twin panels of wrought iron, each bearing the Veymar family crest in hammered bronze. **DM Note:** The guards are professional but human. They've been standing here for three hours. One is younger and more by-the-book; the other is a veteran who just wants the night to end.

**NPC Dialogue — If the party approaches:**

> *The younger one straightens up. "Names on the list, ser. Lord Veymar's orders — no exceptions."*

**3. Scaling the Wall (West Side)**

> *The west side of the estate backs up against a row of townhouses, their upper windows dark. The stone wall here is old — older than the rest of the estate, probably part of the original property before the Veymars rebuilt. Rough-cut blocks, thick with ivy, the mortar crumbling in places. Twelve feet high, give or take. Beyond the wall, you can see the dark shapes of garden topiaries and hear the faint trickle of a fountain. Up on the roof of the estate's west wing, a figure leans against a chimney — an archer, silhouetted against the sky, a longbow slung across his back. He's watching the garden, but every few seconds his gaze sweeps the wall.*

**DM Note:** The Rogue is the natural pick here — Stealth expertise makes the DC 14 comfortable. Oda in spider form can bypass the Athletics check entirely (spider climb). If Jean tries to climb in his plate armor, he has disadvantage on the Athletics check — he's going to clank. Keep him away from this route.

- **Check:** Athletics DC 14 (the wall is 12 feet, rough stone) + Stealth DC 14 (a rooftop archer has line of sight).

**Success:**

> *Your fingers find the gaps in the mortar — one handhold, then two, then your foot catches on a ridge of ivy-thick stone and you pull yourself up. The wall bites cold into your palms. At the top, you flatten yourself against the capstones and wait. The archer on the roof turns, scans the far side of the garden, and you drop — silent, controlled — into a bed of soft earth and trimmed lavender. The scent rises around you, sharp and clean. Above, the archer turns back. He sees nothing. The garden stretches ahead, dark and inviting.*

- Over the wall, into the gardens.

**Failure (Athletics):**

> *Your fingers slip. The ivy tears free in a clump, and for a sickening moment you're hanging by one hand, feet scrabbling against smooth stone — and then you're falling. The ground meets you hard, knocking the wind from your lungs. You lie there for a moment, staring up at the wall that just beat you, the taste of dirt in your mouth.*

- Fall, take 1d6 bludgeoning, must retry or find another way.

**Failure (Stealth):**

> *You're halfway up when you hear it — the creak of a bow being drawn. The archer on the roof has turned, and he's looking right at you, framed against the wall like a fly on a tablecloth. He doesn't shoot. He doesn't have to. He puts two fingers to his lips and lets out a whistle that cuts through the garden like a knife.*

- The archer raises an alarm. +1 Heat.

**4. The Sewers (Beneath the Estate)**

> *The entrance to the sewer system is a rusted iron grate set into the cobblestones of a back alley, two blocks south of the estate. When you pull it open, the smell hits you first — wet stone, stagnant water, and something organic and deeply unpleasant that you don't want to think too hard about. A rusted ladder descends into darkness. Below, you can hear the echo of running water and the occasional skittering of things that live where the light doesn't reach. The tunnels are old — pre-Veymar, pre-Sea District, maybe pre-Waterdeep. They go everywhere. Including, if your sense of direction holds, directly beneath the estate.*

**DM Note:** Lómë is well-suited for this route — CON +2 handles the DC 12, Religion +6 might help her recognize old temple carvings in the tunnel walls (flavor only), and Darkvision means she doesn't need a torch. The Rogue can navigate (Survival or Thieves' Tools proficiency for reading the tunnel layout). If anyone with low CON takes this route, warn them: *"The smell gets worse the deeper you go. Much worse."*

- **Check:** Survival DC 13 (navigating the tunnels) + Constitution DC 12 (the smell is atrocious).

**NPC Encounter — Optional Flavor:** If you want to add tension, have the party encounter a half-mad sewer worker (not hostile, just startling) who mumbles directions before shuffling off into the dark:

> *A voice from the dark. "Left at the third fork. Don't take the right — that one floods." A figure shuffles past you in the ankle-deep water, carrying a hooded lantern that throws wild shadows on the walls. He doesn't stop, doesn't look at you. "And hold your breath past the old cistern. Trust me."*

**Success:**

> *The tunnels twist and branch, but you keep your bearings — left, then straight, then down a set of ancient stone steps that are slick with something you refuse to examine. The air gets colder, cleaner, and then you see it: a wooden trapdoor in the ceiling, light seeping through the cracks. You push it open and the smell of aged oak and fermented grapes floods your senses. Wine racks. Hundreds of bottles, resting in stone alcoves, stretching back into the dark. You're in the cellar. You're beneath the estate. And nobody knows you're here.*

- Emerge in the basement wine cellar. Bypasses all exterior security.

**Failure (Survival):**

> *Every tunnel looks the same — wet stone, brackish water, the same dripping echo from every direction. You double back twice, take a wrong turn at what might have been the third fork or the fourth, and waste precious time retracing your steps through water that's getting deeper. By the time you find the right trapdoor, you've lost almost an hour.*

- Lost for a while — lose time. No Heat, but one less round of exploration in the mansion.

**Failure (Constitution):**

> *The old cistern is the worst of it. The water here is black and still, and the stench that rises from it is something primal — your body rebels before your mind catches up. Your stomach heaves. Your eyes water. You press on, but the damage is done. The taste sits in the back of your throat, and every breath feels like swallowing something rotten. You can't shake it.*

- Poisoned condition for 1 hour (disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks). Nasty.

**NPC Encounter — The Mastiffs:**

They just stop, twenty feet away, and stare at you with the flat, unblinking focus of animals that have been trained to decide very quickly whether you're a threat. One of them lowers its head. - **Check:** Stealth DC 14 (hedge maze has 2 Mastiffs patrolling — Animal Handling DC 15 to calm them, or Stealth to avoid).

**Success (Animal Handling):**

> *You lower yourself slowly — no sudden movements — and extend a hand, palm down. The lead mastiff approaches, stiff-legged, ears flat. It sniffs your hand. Its nostrils flare. For a long, taut moment, nothing happens. Then the tail moves — just once, a slow wag. The second dog follows its partner's lead, and within a minute they're both pressing their heads against your legs, tongues lolling, the threat forgotten. > *You press yourself into the hedge wall and hold your breath. One of the mastiffs passes within arm's reach — close enough to feel the heat of its body, to hear the wet rhythm of its breathing. You slip through the gap it left and reach the conservatory door without a sound.*

**Failure:**

> *The lead mastiff throws its head back and lets out a bark that echoes off the estate walls like a thunderclap. The second joins in — a deep, booming alarm that shreds the quiet of the garden. Lights flare in the guard house. You hear voices, boots on gravel, the metallic ring of weapons being drawn. The dogs don't chase you. They don't have to. They've done their job.*

- Dogs bark. +1 Heat. Guards investigate the conservatory.

### Transition — You're Inside. Now What?

Once the party is inside — whether through one entrance or split across several — read the appropriate passage based on where they entered:

**If they entered through the front (Main Gate, Fake Invitation, or Performer's Entrance):**

> *The warmth of the ballroom envelops you. Candlelight catches on crystal and silver. The string quartet has shifted into something slow and stately, and a hundred conversations blend into a single, thrumming hum. Servants weave between guests with trays of champagne and canapés. No one is looking at you. Everyone is looking at everyone else. Somewhere above you — up past the grand staircase, past the second-floor gallery, behind a locked door on the third floor — is a puzzle box that someone is paying you a lot of gold to steal.*
>
> *The clock in the foyer reads half past nine. Dawn is a long way off. But the night isn't going to wait for you.*

**If they entered through the kitchen or servant's entrance:**

> *The kitchen corridor is narrow, hot, and loud. Steam hisses from somewhere, and a cook shouts an order that nobody acknowledges. You press against the wall as a servant rushes past with a tureen of soup, and then you're through — into a quieter hallway, dimly lit, with a servants' staircase rising to the left and the distant sound of music drifting from the ballroom to the right. You are backstage in someone else's house. Every door you open from here is a gamble.*

**If they entered through the wine cellar (Sewers or Conservatory Passage):**

> *The wine cellar is cold and silent after the chaos of getting here. Racks of dusty bottles stretch into the dark, and the stone floor is dry and clean. A single staircase leads up — narrow, wooden, the steps worn smooth by decades of servants hauling bottles to dinner parties. At the top, you can see a sliver of light beneath a closed door. The muffled sounds of the gala filter down: music, laughter, the clink of glass. You're beneath the heart of the estate. Everything is above you now.*

**If they entered through the gardens (Scaling the Wall):**

> *The garden is darker than you expected. Topiaries loom like sentinels, and the fountain gurgles somewhere to your left. The ballroom windows blaze with light on the far side of the grounds — you can see figures moving behind the glass, silhouettes dancing. Between you and the house: fifty yards of open lawn, a gravel path that will crunch underfoot, and the occasional sweep of a lantern from the upper windows. The back door is there. You just have to reach it.*

**DM Note:** If the party split up to use different entrances, this is the moment to reunite them. Ask each group: *"Where are you heading?"* The natural convergence point is the **ground floor main hallway** — it connects the kitchen, the ballroom, and the main foyer. Or, if one group went through the cellar and another through the front, have them run into each other on the servants' staircase for a tense, funny moment of mutual recognition. Once everyone is inside and oriented, move to Act 4.

### Setting the Tone

> *The inside of the Veymar estate hits you like stepping into another world. The ground floor hums with life — warm candlelight reflected in polished marble, the low murmur of a hundred overlapping conversations, the clink of crystal, the perfume of hothouse flowers competing with roasted meat and mulled wine. Somewhere deeper in the house, the string quartet shifts into a waltz, and the crowd responds like a living thing — bodies turning, dresses swirling, laughter rising and falling like waves.*
>
> *But beneath all of it — beneath the music and the chatter and the warm golden light — there's the constant, quiet movement of people who are watching. A guard in polished half-plate stationed by the main staircase. A servant whose eyes linger a half-second too long. The flash of a captain's insignia disappearing around a corner.*
>
> *This place is beautiful. It is also a cage with very attentive keepers.*

### Three Paths to the Study

**Jean's Passive Perception is 9.** He will walk past traps, wards, and clues without noticing. The other three (Martin, Lómë, Oda at passive 16) need to spot things and share info. This creates natural party dynamics — the observant characters guide the confident but oblivious paladin.

Players will likely combine approaches. Present these as the natural options based on where they entered:

---

**Path A — Through the Ballroom (Social)**

Mingle with guests, gather info, work their way upstairs.

> *The ballroom is a cathedral of vanity. Three crystal chandeliers hang from a vaulted ceiling painted with scenes of Waterdhavian conquest — ships, dragons, men in armor striking heroic poses. Below them, a sea of silk and velvet swirls across a floor of black-and-white marble tile. The string quartet plays on a raised platform near the west windows, framed by curtains of deep crimson. Servants weave through the crowd carrying silver trays of wine and tiny, architectural food — the kind that looks like a jewel and tastes like a promise.*
>
> *At the far end of the ballroom, a grand staircase sweeps upward to the second floor. A single guard stands at the bottom, hands clasped behind his back, watching the crowd with the bored patience of a man who does this every week.*

**Checks:** Deception or Performance DC 13 (blend in), Persuasion DC 14 (get a noble to vouch for them or distract guards), Stealth DC 14 (slip upstairs unnoticed during a toast or performance).

**DM Note:** Martin has +7 Performance — this is his room. If the party entered via the Performer's Entrance, he's already got a reason to be on the stage. Let him own this. Jean's Knight background means he knows how to hold a wine glass and nod at the right moments, even if his Passive Perception means he's oblivious to the guard counting heads.

**The Nosy Noble — Lord Pemberton:**

Within a few minutes of mingling, a noble approaches whoever looks most out of place (probably Lómë or the Rogue, unless they're well-disguised).

> *A portly man in a burgundy doublet waddles over with the unstoppable momentum of someone who has never been told to go away. His face is flushed — he's at least four glasses of wine into the evening — and his smile is the kind that expects you to be delighted by his attention.*
>
> *"I say! I don't believe I've had the pleasure. Lord Desmond Pemberton. I know everyone, of course — it's rather my thing — and I'm quite certain I don't know you. Which means you must be terribly interesting, or terribly lost."*
>
> *He laughs at his own joke. It goes on too long.*

**DM Note:** Pemberton is harmless but loud. He's a social obstacle, not a threat. The danger is that he draws attention and won't shut up. A Persuasion DC 12 redirects him ("Lord Pemberton, is that Lady Gralhund by the punch bowl? I believe she was looking for you."). A Performance DC 11 entertains him enough that he becomes an unwitting ally ("You simply MUST meet Captain Helm — she's always so serious, she needs someone like you to liven things up!" — this is a trap; avoid it). Ignoring him or being rude doesn't raise Heat, but he'll loudly complain about the "odd newcomers" within earshot of a guard.

**Lady Thessaly — The Tipsy Socialite:**

If players reach the area near the grand staircase, or if they're looking for an ally, Lady Thessaly finds them. She's been drinking steadily since the gala began.

> *A woman in an emerald gown detaches herself from a cluster of laughing nobles and drifts toward you. She's striking — high cheekbones, dark hair pinned with silver combs, the kind of beauty that's been sharpened by years of smiling at people she despises. She holds her wine glass like a weapon.*
>
> *"You look like you're actually interesting. Do you know how rare that is at one of these things?"*
>
> *She takes a long sip of wine and looks you over with an appraiser's eye.*
>
> *"I'm Thessaly. Lady Thessaly, technically, though the 'Lady' part is mostly a formality at this point. Aldric — Lord Veymar, my husband — is upstairs doing whatever it is he does when he disappears for hours. Probably talking to that box again."*

> *She says this lightly, but her jaw tightens.*
>
> *"He used to talk to me. Now it's the box. Always the box. Tell me — do you find it normal for a grown man to whisper to an object?"*

**DM Note:** Thessaly is bitter, lonely, and three glasses deep. She's not an enemy — she genuinely resents Veymar's obsession. If treated with sympathy (Persuasion DC 12 or just good RP), she'll share useful info: the study is on the third floor, Veymar locks himself in every evening, the stairwell is locked, Captain Helm carries the only spare key. She'll even create a distraction if asked — but not consciously help them steal from her husband. Frame it as: "She might drunkenly cause a scene near the staircase guard if someone suggests her husband is ignoring her at his own party." No check required — just say the right thing and she'll do the rest.

If Lómë or Jean approach with genuine compassion (not manipulation), Thessaly opens up more:

> *She stares into her wine for a moment.*
>
> *"He wasn't always like this, you know. Before the box, he was just... eccentric. A collector. Harmless. But ever since that shipwreck haul three months ago — he doesn't sleep. He barely eats. His eyes have this..." She trails off. "Never mind. You didn't come here to listen to a wife complain."*

> *She straightens, and the socialite mask snaps back into place.*
>

**Encounter (Heat 1+): Captain Helm Approaches**

**Path C — Outside Route (Acrobatic)**

Scale balconies and rooftops to reach the third-floor balcony directly.

> *The night air hits you like cold water after the warmth of the estate. Above, a half-moon hangs in a sky streaked with thin clouds. Below, the gardens are dark — hedge rows and gravel paths silvered with moonlight. Somewhere down there, a mastiff whines and is shushed by its handler.*

> *The second-floor balcony is twelve feet above you — weathered stone with iron railings, the kind of balcony that exists so wealthy people can stare at their own gardens. Beyond it, the rooftop stretches toward the third floor like a tilted field of dark slate tiles. From up here, you can see the third-floor balcony — a narrow ledge of stone railing and a glass door, maybe forty feet along the roofline.*
>
> *The wind picks up. A tile somewhere above shifts and clicks against its neighbor.*

**Checks:** Athletics DC 14 (climb to second-floor balcony), Acrobatics DC 15 (traverse rooftop to third-floor balcony), Stealth DC 14 (avoid the rooftop archer).

**The Climb:**

On success (Athletics):

> *Your fingers find the cracks between the stones — old mortar, crumbling in places, solid enough in others. You pull yourself up hand over hand, boots scraping against ivy that tears away in strips beneath your weight. For one bad moment, your foot slips and you swing by your fingers, dangling twelve feet above the gravel path. Then your hand finds the iron railing and you haul yourself over, landing on the balcony in a crouch.*
>
> *Below, a guard walks past on the garden path. He doesn't look up. People never look up.*

On failure (Athletics): Fall, take 1d6 bludgeoning, must retry or find another way. The noise may attract attention (Stealth DC 12 to stay quiet on impact, or +1 Heat).

**The Rooftop Crossing:**

> *The rooftop is a nightmare of angles. Slate tiles slick with evening dew, pitched at a grade steep enough that every step wants to become a slide. The wind is stronger up here — it tugs at your clothes, pushes at your balance, carries the muffled sound of the gala below like music from another life. The third-floor balcony waits at the far end, a dark rectangle against the pale stone.*
>
> *Between you and it: forty feet of tilted roof, a chimney stack, and the certain knowledge that a fall from this height will be loud, painful, and extremely difficult to explain.*

On success (Acrobatics):

> *You move in a low crouch, weight on your toes, fingers trailing against the tiles for balance. Each step is deliberate — test the tile, shift your weight, move. The chimney stack gives you a handhold at the halfway point, and you press your back against the warm brick and breathe. > *Then you push off and cover the last twenty feet to the third-floor balcony. Your hands close around the stone railing and you pull yourself over, landing softly on the narrow ledge. The glass door is right there — dark, quiet, waiting.*

**Encounter (Heat 1+): The Rooftop Archer**

The real tension is anyone with lower Stealth (Jean at +0 should NOT be on this path — but if he insists, make the dice roll dramatic).

**Note on Jean's weapons:** His Warhorse and Mounted Combatant feat are useless inside the mansion. His lance has disadvantage within 5 ft without a mount. > *The second-floor corridor narrows as you move away from the guest rooms and toward the back of the house. The carpet thins, then disappears entirely. The wallpaper changes — from silk damask to bare plaster. The gala is a distant murmur now, two floors and a world away.*
>
> *At the end of the corridor, set into an alcove lit by a single guttering candle, is a heavy oak door reinforced with iron bands. A brass lock — newer than the door, recently installed — gleams in the candlelight. Beyond this door, a narrow staircase leads up to the third floor.*
>

**Picking the Lock — The Tension Beat:**

> *The pick slides into the lock. The first tumbler clicks — loud in the silence — and you freeze. Nothing. No shout, no footsteps. You exhale and go back to work.*
>
> *The second tumbler resists. You can feel it catching on something — a false groove, the kind a locksmith adds to frustrate exactly this kind of thing. The footsteps below are getting louder. Someone is coming up the main staircase.*
>
> *The third tumbler. Your fingers are steady but your pulse isn't. The footsteps reach the second-floor landing. A voice — a guard, calling to someone: "Anything up here?"*
>
> *Click.*

On success:

> *The lock turns. The door swings inward on oiled hinges — someone keeps this door well-maintained, even if they never want it opened. Beyond, a narrow staircase spirals upward into darkness.*
>
> *Behind you, the guard's voice again, closer: "No? Right. Check the library." Footsteps recede. You slip through the door and pull it shut behind you.*
>
> *You're through.*

On failure (Thieves' Tools): The lock doesn't budge. They can retry (takes another minute — roll again, but the patrol is closer) or find another way. Each retry increases the chance of discovery — after two failures, a guard rounds the corner unless the party has set up a distraction.

The contrast between the noisy, golden gala below and this cold, quiet floor should feel wrong. Something is off about the third floor. Something has been off for three months, ever since Veymar brought the box home. Let them feel it before they open the study door and move into Act 5.

## Act 5 — Retrieving the Puzzle Box (~15-20 min)

### Approach to the Study

> *The staircase to the third floor is narrower than the ones below — older, too. The carpet thins out, the wallpaper darkens from cream to a deep, bruised burgundy. The noise of the gala drops away with every step, replaced by a silence that feels deliberate, like the house itself is holding its breath. The air up here is different — cooler, drier, and carrying a faint metallic taste at the back of your throat, like the moment before a lightning strike.*
>
> *The hallway stretches ahead of you, lit by a single oil lamp on a wall sconce that barely pushes back the dark. Closed doors on either side. At the far end — the study. Even from here, you can see the door is heavier than the others. Reinforced oak, iron banding. The kind of door that says: this room matters.*

**DM Note:** Give the players a moment here. This is the first time the gala noise has completely vanished. Let them feel the quiet. If anyone asks about the other doors — the workshop door on the right is slightly ajar (warm candlelight visible through the gap), and the gallery door on the left is locked (Thieves' Tools DC 12). Neither room contains the box, but both reward investigation with clues about Veymar and the artifact. See the next two sections. Don't push players toward the rooms, but don't shut them down either — the heist clock is ticking, and the choice to spend time here is theirs.

### The Arcane Workshop (If Players Investigate)

**DM Note — Running the Workshop:** The workshop door is ajar — warm candlelight spills into the hallway through the gap. Players don't need a check to enter. This room tells the story of Veymar's desperate three-month attempt to understand and contain the box. The chalk circle on the floor, the burned-out reagents, the frantic journal notes — everything here says: this man has been losing a war against something he can't beat. Tier the reveals by Investigation DC. If they're in a rush, the room description alone (DC 10) is enough atmosphere. If they dig, they get foreshadowing that pays off in Act 6.

**When they enter (no check required):**

> *The door swings open at a touch — it wasn't even latched. The room beyond is smaller than you expected. Stone flagstones instead of hardwood, rough and cold underfoot. A heavy workbench runs along the far wall, its surface crowded with glass vials, brass instruments, and curled scrolls. Two candles burn in iron holders on the bench — still lit, wax pooled thick around their bases. Someone was here recently.*
>
> *The air smells of sulfur, dried herbs, and something acrid — like burned copper. A half-eaten bread roll and a cold cup of tea sit on a small writing desk in the corner, next to a brass microscope and a stack of leather journals. And on the floor, near the center of the room — a circle. Drawn in white chalk on the grey stone, partially scuffed and smudged, with symbols at five points around its circumference. The chalk dust is ground into the stone. This circle has been drawn, erased, and redrawn many times.*

- **Investigation DC 13** — Closer examination of the workbench and chalk circle. The Rogue's Investigation or any character spending a minute looking carefully.

> *The workbench tells a story of failure. Half the vials are empty or shattered — scorch marks on the stone beneath them where something went wrong. Formulas are scratched into the wood surface with a knife point, then crossed out, then scratched again. One phrase is gouged deep enough to splinter the wood: DOES NOT YIELD.*
>
> *The chalk circle on the floor is a containment diagram — you've seen simpler versions in spellbooks. This one is ambitious and sloppy, copied from a source the artist didn't fully understand. The five symbols at the compass points are ritual anchors, but two of them are drawn wrong — the strokes go in the wrong direction, which would collapse the containment the moment any real power touched it. Whoever drew this is educated but desperate. They're trying something far beyond their skill.*

- **Investigation or Arcana DC 16** — The journal on the writing desk. Martin (Arcana +7) or anyone with Investigation expertise is best suited for this.

> *The topmost journal is open to a page dense with tight, slanted handwriting — the script of a man writing too fast to be careful. You scan the entries:*
>
> *"Day 34. Tried silver nitrate solution on the outer seal. No reaction. Tried again with blood — my blood. The patterns on the surface moved. They MOVED. The seal responded to the blood but did not open. It wants more."*
>
> *"Day 51. I understand the geometry now. Five points. Five anchors. The seal is a blood ritual — it requires five lives, freely given or freely taken. This is not a lock. It is a covenant. I cannot break it alone, and I will not break it at all."*
>
> *"Day 78. I have stopped trying to open it. I am now trying to ensure it can never BE opened. Every ward I place on the study dissolves within days. The box consumes protective magic like kindling. It is patient. It is waiting for the right person to find it. I am not that person. God help whoever is."*

**DM Note — What the Journal Means:** This is major foreshadowing. "Five lives freely given or freely taken" directly prefigures the Act 6 ritual — and Locke hired exactly five people. If a player makes that connection now, that's perfect. Don't confirm or deny it. Let them sit with the dread. Veymar's line "I will not break it at all" also reframes him from obstacle to tragic figure — he's been trying to protect the world, not hoard power.

- **Investigation DC 19** — A loose page half-hidden under a beaker on the workbench. This is hard to find — only a thorough search turns it up.

> *Tucked beneath a heavy glass beaker, a single loose page — different from the journal, unlined, the kind used for sketching. A charcoal drawing, hasty but precise: a figure in a long coat standing in this very room. Well-dressed. Poised. The face is left vague — just the suggestion of a smile — but the hands are rendered in detail, as if the artist kept coming back to them.*
>
> *Beneath the sketch, a note in the same tight handwriting: "The broker came again. Offered to 'relieve me of my burden.' His courtesy is practiced — every word measured, every gesture rehearsed. I do not trust him. Something about his hands."*

- **Perception DC 19** (on the sketch) — Notice the detail in the sketch. This is the subtle Locke hint — the hands are drawn backward (thumbs on the outside). Players who remember Locke's gloves may make the connection.

> *You look closer at the hands in the sketch — and something is wrong. The thumbs. They're on the outside of the hands, not the inside. The fingers curve the wrong way, as if the hands were put on backward. It could be a mistake — a hasty sketch drawn from memory. But the rest of the drawing is careful. Deliberate. The artist drew what he saw.*

**DM Tip — The Backward Hands:** This is Locke. Rakshasa have reversed hands — a detail Veymar noticed but couldn't explain. If a player connects this to Locke's ever-present gloves from Act 1, reward them with a knowing nod. Don't spell it out. If nobody catches it, that's fine — the Act 6 reveal works either way. But if someone goes pale at the table, you've earned it.

### The Private Gallery (If Players Investigate)

**DM Note — Running the Gallery:** The gallery door is locked — Thieves' Tools DC 12, trivial for the Rogue. This room is the counterpoint to the workshop: it shows who Veymar was before the box. **Once inside:**

A narrow wooden bench runs down the center of the room, its cushion flattened and faded from years of use. A brass oil lamp on the wall is cold and dark — it hasn't been lit in a long time. Dust covers everything. The bench, the frames, the floor. A thin, undisturbed layer that says: no one has set foot in this room in weeks. Maybe months.*

The painting and sextant are physical evidence tying the box to a specific shipwreck, which players may want to investigate later (there is no later — the heist clock is ticking — but the detail makes the world feel real).

- **History or Religion DC 16** — Examine the oldest painting in the gallery — the dark one at the far wall. Lómë gets advantage on the Religion version (Kelemvor iconography).

> *The oldest painting hangs in the darkest corner, furthest from the door. It's large — three feet across — and so dark with age that you almost missed it. The oils have deepened to near-black, but when you lean close, the scene emerges from the murk like something surfacing from deep water.*

Their hands are raised, palms outward, and between them — suspended in the air above the altar — a small dark object pulses with painted light. **DM Note — The Painting and the Ritual:** This painting depicts the original sealing of the Pashupatastra fragment — the same ritual Locke will attempt to reverse in Act 6. If the players found the workshop journal ("five lives freely given or freely taken") AND see this painting, the pieces fit together: the five-pointed chalk circle, the five-soul seal, the five members of their party. Don't narrate the connection — let them make it themselves. If they do, the dread is earned.

- **Arcana or Investigation DC 19** — Examine the barnacle-encrusted sextant from the shipwreck. Martin (Arcana +7) is best suited.

> *You pick up the sextant — heavier than it looks, the brass green and rough beneath the barnacles. On impulse, you brush your sleeve across the flat of the instrument, clearing away a crust of dried salt and sea growth. And there, etched into the brass beneath the grime — sigils. Tiny, precise, geometric. The same shifting patterns you saw on the surface of the puzzle box.*
>
> *This isn't a navigation instrument. It's a ward. The sigils are a charm of perpetual motion — designed to keep something moving, to prevent it from remaining in one place for too long. The box was never supposed to arrive anywhere. It was supposed to drift — shipwreck to shipwreck, current to current, forever lost at sea.*
>
> *Until Veymar pulled it from the water and brought it home.*

**DM Tip — The Box Was Meant to Stay Lost:** This is the final piece of the gallery's story: the Pashupatastra fragment wasn't just sealed — it was set adrift. The sextant-ward was meant to keep it moving through the ocean forever, beyond the reach of anyone who might try to open it. Veymar broke that chain by salvaging the wreck. He didn't just find the box — he ended centuries of deliberate concealment. No wonder it's been consuming him ever since.

### The Arcane Ward

The study door has an **Arcane Ward** (if the players learned about this from Lord Thorne, they're prepared; if not, it's a surprise).

- **Detection:** Detect Magic or Arcana DC 14 reveals the ward. Three casters have Detect Magic (Martin, Lome, Oda) — this will be spotted. That's OK; it rewards preparation.

**If someone casts Detect Magic or succeeds on Arcana DC 14:**

> *The moment you extend your senses toward the door, you see it — a lattice of pale blue-white threads woven across the door frame like a spider's web made of starlight. The lines pulse faintly, rhythmically, like a heartbeat. The magic is dense and layered — not crude alarm work, but something designed by someone who understood the craft. At the center of the web, just above the door frame, a small rune stone is set into the wall, almost invisible unless you know to look. That's the anchor.*

**If nobody checks and they just try the door:** The ward is invisible to the naked eye. They trigger it with no warning.

- **Bypass:** Dispel Magic (DC 13 — Martin auto-succeeds), Thieves' Tools DC 16 (the ward has a physical anchor — a rune stone above the door frame; the Rogue likely auto-passes with expertise), or Arcana DC 16 (carefully unravel the enchantment).

**On successful bypass (Dispel Magic):**

> *Martin speaks the counterspell and the threads of light shiver, contract, and dissolve like frost in sunlight. The rune stone above the door frame cracks down the middle with a soft tick — barely louder than a fingernail against glass. The door is just a door now. The hallway exhales.*

**On successful bypass (Thieves' Tools):**

> *The Rogue reaches up, tools in hand, and works the rune stone loose from the wall with surgical precision — a twist, a scrape, a final careful pull. The stone comes free trailing a wisp of fading light like smoke from a blown-out candle. The web unravels from the edges inward, and in three seconds, the ward is gone. Clean work.*

**On successful bypass (Arcana DC 16):**

> *You press your hands close to the web of light — not touching, never touching — and begin teasing the strands apart, redirecting the flow of magic back into the rune stone until it overloads and goes dark. It's like defusing a bomb made of thought. The last thread snaps, and the doorway clears.*

- **Trigger:** If they open the door without bypassing the ward, a silent **Alarm** spell notifies Veymar. He will arrive in 3 rounds with 2 Guards. +1 Heat (or instant to Heat 2 if not already).

**If the ward is triggered:**

> *The moment the door opens, the lattice of invisible threads collapses inward — and for a single heartbeat, you feel it: a pulse of magic that passes through you like a cold wind, outward and downward, through the floors, through the walls, aimed at something — someone — below. There's no sound. No flash of light. Nothing visible at all. But every caster in the group feels their skin prickle with the unmistakable sensation of a message being sent.*
>
> *Someone downstairs just found out you're here.*

**DM Note:** Don't tell them explicitly that Veymar is coming. Let them feel the dread. Start a mental 3-round timer. If they're fast, they can grab the box and go. If they dawdle, Veymar arrives at the study door with guards.

### Getting the Box

**Option A — The Glass Display Case (Default)**

The box sits in a glass case on an iron pedestal.

> *The display case is simple — clear glass on an iron frame, no lock. The box sits on a velvet cushion inside, perfectly centered. It looks easy. It looks like you could just reach in and take it.*

- **Trap:** The pedestal is pressure-sensitive. Removing the box without disabling the mechanism triggers a **Glyph of Warding** (3d8 thunder damage, DC 14 Dex save for half, VERY LOUD — instant Heat 2).

**Discovery — Investigation DC 14 (the Rogue's moment):**

> *You lean in close — not touching, just looking. And you see it. The velvet cushion sits slightly lower than it should, compressed by the weight of the box. Beneath the cushion, through the glass base, you can just make out a thin copper plate — a pressure switch. And etched into the underside of that plate, a glyph. You've seen this kind of work before. Lift the box without counterweight, and the plate rises. The glyph fires. Everyone in this room has a very bad night.*

- **Disable:** Thieves' Tools DC 15 (likely trivial for the Rogue with expertise), or Investigation DC 14 to spot the mechanism + Sleight of Hand DC 15 to swap the box's weight with something equivalent (~2 lbs). If the Rogue isn't here (bad party split), it falls to Martin (Investigation +4, Sleight of Hand +3 — risky). **Make sure the Rogue is on the infiltration team, not the social team.**

**The Moment of Taking — If they disable the trap and lift the box:**

> *The glass case opens silently. Your hand closes around the puzzle box and — it's warm. Not like something left in the sun. Warm like something alive. The geometric patterns on its surface shift under your fingers, adjusting, rearranging, as if the box is aware it's been moved. It weighs almost nothing. It feels like holding a clenched fist.*
>
> *The pedestal stays flat. The glyph stays dark. You have it.*

**The Heist-Movie Swap — If they use Sleight of Hand to swap the weight:**

> *One hand on the box. The other holding the counterweight — [whatever the player chose]. You breathe in. You breathe out. And in a single fluid motion, you make the swap. Box up. Weight down. The copper plate doesn't move. The glyph doesn't fire. The room stays silent.*
>
> *For one perfect second, you're the best thief in Waterdeep.*

**DM Note:** If the Rogue does the swap, let them describe what they use as counterweight. A wine bottle from downstairs, a heavy book from the shelf, a bag of coins — whatever they pick, say yes and let them have the moment. This is their set piece.

- **Brute Force:** Smash the case (AC 13, 3 HP) — triggers the glyph.

**If the trap triggers:**

> *The copper plate snaps upward. The glyph blazes white-hot. And the world becomes sound — a concussive boom of raw thunder that blows the glass case apart, sends books tumbling from shelves, and slams through your chest like a fist. The windows rattle in their frames. Dust falls from the ceiling. Your ears are ringing — and below you, through the floorboards, you can hear shouting. A lot of shouting.*

3d8 thunder damage, DC 14 Dex save for half. Instant Heat 2. Every guard in the estate heard that.

**Option B — Veymar Has It On Him (Alternative)**

If the players got specific intel suggesting Veymar keeps it close (or if you want an alternate challenge), the box is in his **breast pocket** during the gala.

> *You spot Veymar near the far end of the ballroom, holding a glass of wine he hasn't touched, listening to a conversation he clearly finds tedious. - **Pickpocket:** Sleight of Hand DC 18 (he's paranoid and alert).
- **Distraction + Pickpocket:** Create a scene (Lady Thessaly can help if befriended) to drop the DC to 14.
- **Confrontation:** Corner him privately. Intimidation DC 18 or combat (use Noble stat block with 2 Guards).

**If confronted privately, Veymar speaks:**

> *Veymar's composure doesn't break — it freezes. His hand closes around the shape in his pocket and his eyes go flat and hard.*
>
> *"Do you have any idea what you're trying to steal? Do you even know what it is?" He shakes his head slowly, the way you'd look at a child reaching for a hot stove. "This isn't treasure. This isn't some trinket. Whoever sent you — whoever told you to take this — they didn't tell you the truth. They couldn't have. >
> *His voice drops to a whisper.*
>

**DM Note:** This is genuine. Veymar is paranoid and arrogant, but he's not wrong about the box. If players hesitate, let them — it creates dramatic tension. But the job is the job. They can wrestle with it after.

The box radiates overwhelming abjuration and evocation magic — far more powerful than anything Lord Veymar could create. The seal is not mechanical — it's **blood magic**, ancient and primal.

The seal requires a specific ritual to break. Something about *"five lives freely given... or freely taken."* The magic inside the box feels like standing at the edge of a furnace — not fire, but *annihilation*. Whatever is inside was not made by mortal hands. (This is the foreshadowing payoff.)

## Act 6 — The Escape & The Finale (~30-40 min)

### Part 1: Getting Out

**If Heat is 0-1 (Unnoticed/Suspicious) — Clean Exit**

> **Read Aloud:**
> *You retrace your steps through the mansion — back down the narrow stairwell, through corridors now emptied of all but the most dedicated partygoers. >
> *The night air hits you as you slip through the servants' entrance — cool and salt-sharp, carrying the distant creak of ships at anchor. Behind you, the Veymar estate glows like a lantern, oblivious. The puzzle box is a hard weight in your pack.*
>
> *You did it. You actually did it.*

Simple Stealth DC 12 check to avoid a last-minute patrol. Failure means a brief tense moment but nothing more — they make it out.

**If Heat is 2 (Alarmed) — Arcane Lockdown**

> **Read Aloud:**
> *A deep hum reverberates through the walls. The windows flash with blue-white light as arcane sigils bloom across every door frame and window in the mansion. The exits are sealed. From below, you hear Captain Helm's voice, clipped and furious: "Lock it down. Nobody leaves."*

**Skill Challenge: Escape the Estate (4 successes before 3 failures)**

Each player describes their action. Assign a DC 14-16 check based on their approach. Examples:
- Dispel Magic on a sealed door (automatic success — costs a spell slot)
- Athletics to smash through a window (DC 15, takes 1d6 slashing damage)
- Arcana to temporarily suppress the ward on an exit (DC 16)
- Stealth to evade a guard patrol in the chaos (DC 14)
- Deception to impersonate a panicking noble and get guards to open a door (DC 15)

**On success (4 before 3):** They escape through a gap in the lockdown.
**On failure (3 before 4):** They escape but are chased. 4 Guards intercept them on the grounds. Quick combat or one final group Stealth DC 15 to lose the pursuit in the Sea District streets.

### Part 2: The Drop-Off — The Betrayal

> **Read Aloud:**
> *Locke's instructions lead you to a warehouse at the far edge of the Dock District — the kind of building that looks abandoned on purpose. The door is unlocked. Inside, the space has been cleared except for a large circle etched into the stone floor in what looks like dried blood. Five points of the circle are marked with iron braziers, already lit with pale blue flame. Locke stands at the center, still wearing that calm, faintly amused expression.*
>
> *"You have it. Good. You've done well." He extends a gloved hand. "The box, please."*

**The Ritual Circle:**
Players with Arcana DC 12 or passive Perception 14+ notice:
- The circle has **five points**. There are **five** of them.
- The braziers ignited as they entered — they didn't see Locke light them.
- The "dried blood" in the circle's lines is fresh underneath.

Three PCs have Passive Perception 16 (Martin, Lómë, Oda) — they **automatically** notice all of this. Multiple characters realizing simultaneously creates a great dramatic moment. Describe worried glances, hands drifting to weapons, a shared unspoken "something is wrong." Give the party a beat to react before Locke speaks.

**If the Party Refuses to Enter:** This party has high Perception and multiple magic-sensitive characters. There's a real chance they stop at the warehouse door.

If they hesitate: Locke steps forward, still in disguise, hands open. *"What's wrong? You have the box. I have your gold. Let's finish this."* The braziers sit outside the main circle — they appear decorative. An Arcana DC 16 check reveals the circle is magical (Lómë has +6, Martin gets +3 from Jack of All Trades).

If they refuse to enter: Locke grows impatient. The reveal happens at the warehouse entrance instead — he drops the disguise, and the 2 Cult Fanatics emerge from behind crates to push the party toward the circle by force. The fight begins immediately with **no ritual active** (the ritual only activates once all 5 PCs are inside the circle, or Locke triggers it manually as a last resort — in which case it requires a full round to activate and deals only 2d8 necrotic instead of 3d8, representing an imperfect binding).

**The Reveal:**

When they hand over the box (or if they hesitate and Locke grows impatient):

### Part 3: The Final Battle

**Round 1 — The Ritual**
At initiative count 20 (losing ties), the ritual circle pulses. Each PC standing inside the circle must make a **DC 15 Charisma saving throw** or take **3d8 necrotic damage** (half on success) as the circle tries to drain their life force. Leaving the circle (moving 15+ feet from center) ends this effect for that PC.

### Party vs. Locke — What You Need to Know

**CRITICAL:** This party maxes out at 3rd-level spell slots. Locke's immunity to "3rd level and below" means he is **immune to EVERY spell the party can cast** at the start of this fight. This includes Counterspell, Fireball, Spirit Guardians, Hold Person, all cantrips — everything.

**What DOES work against Locke (always):**
- **Physical weapon attacks** (if the weapon is magical — see below)
- **Jean's Divine Smite** (delivered via weapon attack, not a spell)
- **Oda's Primal Strike** (beast form attacks count as magical at level 6)
- **Jean's Magic Weapon spell** (cast on his OWN weapon, not on Locke — targets the weapon, not the Rakshasa)
- **Martin's Cutting Words** (Bardic Inspiration feature, not a spell — subtracts d8 from enemy attack/damage/check rolls)
- **Bardic Inspiration, Healing Word, Bless** on allies (these target allies, not Locke)
- **Dispel Magic on a DOMINATED ALLY** (targets the spell effect on the ally, not Locke — see Dominate Person below)
- **Brazier disruption** (Athletics checks — no magic needed)

**Martin's combat role:** Cutting Words to reduce Locke's rolls, Bardic Inspiration to boost allies, Healing Word to keep people up. He has no weapons and his spells can't target Locke. He's the **support MVP** who becomes a powerhouse as braziers fall.

**Locke (Rakshasa) — See stat-blocks.md for full stats**
- Uses his first turn to cast **Dominate Person** on Jean LeMarque (DC 15 Wis save). See "Dominate Person on Jean" below.
- Fights intelligently — he expected this to be easy and grows increasingly frustrated if the PCs fight well.
- If reduced to half HP, snarls: *"Enough! I'll peel the souls from your bodies the old-fashioned way."* Shifts to melee with claw attacks.

### Dominate Person on Jean — The Big Moment

Locke targets Jean — the strongest martial PC with the weakest Wisdom save.

**Jean's WIS save:** -1 (WIS) + 3 (Aura of Protection) = **+2 total.** Needs 13+ on d20. **40% chance to save. 60% chance to be dominated.**

**If dominated:** Jean has Extra Attack + Divine Smite. He can deal 1d8+3 (warhammer) + 2d8 (smite) per hit, twice per round. That's **30-40 damage per round against allies.** With AC 19 and 52 HP, he's incredibly hard to bring down without killing him.

**Locke's brilliant tactic:** Command Jean to move away from the party. The **Aura of Protection moves with Jean** — suddenly the remaining PCs lose their +3 to saves, making the ritual's Charisma save and Locke's other spells much more dangerous. This should feel like a coordinated strike.

**How the party can fight back:**
- **Martin casts Dispel Magic on Jean** (targeting the Dominate Person effect ON Jean, not Locke). Dominate Person is 5th level, so Martin needs an ability check: d20 + 7 (spellcasting modifier) vs DC 15. Needs 8+ — likely to succeed. **This is Martin's most important spell of the fight.**
- **Lómë's Sentinel at Death's Door** negates critical hits from a dominated Jean (3 uses/LR)
- **Oda in beast form can grapple Jean** to limit his movement (beast STR is often very high)
- **Jean gets a new save each time he takes damage** — allies can deliberately deal small amounts (unarmed strikes, 1 damage) to give him chances
- The Rogue can use **Cunning Action** to stay out of Jean's reach

**Roleplay:** Jean's personality is "eloquent flattery" and "hero of the people." Being forced to attack his friends should be horrifying — his mouth says charming things while his body swings the warhammer. Riley should love this dramatic moment.

**Locke's Minions:**
- **2 Cult Fanatics** emerge from the shadows at initiative count 10 in Round 1. They try to keep PCs inside the circle and maintain the ritual.
- Lómë's Spirit Guardians vs. the Cultists' Spirit Guardians creates a dramatic competing-aura battlefield. Note: Lómë's Spirit Guardians won't affect Locke (spell immunity) but will shred the Cultists.
- Cultist Hold Person (DC 11) is less threatening thanks to Jean's Aura — if he's nearby. If he's dominated and walking away, the DC 11 becomes harder for Lómë (+3 WIS save without Aura = needs 8+, still OK).

**Battlefield:**
- The warehouse is 60 ft x 40 ft. The ritual circle is 20 ft diameter in the center.
- Crates and barrels along the walls (half cover).
- **The +1 ornate dagger** is on a crate near the wall — visible to anyone with passive Perception 14+ (three PCs have 16) or Investigation DC 10. This is the **Rogue's weapon** for this fight. Make sure it's findable early. (If Martin still has no equipment, this might be the first weapon he touches all session.)
- **Jean's Warhorse:** The warehouse is tight but a Large creature (10x10 ft) can maneuver. If Jean summons his steed via Find Steed, Mounted Combatant gives him advantage on melee attacks vs. Medium or smaller creatures (Cult Fanatics, but NOT Locke who is also Medium). The lance's reach (10 ft) is useful for staying out of Spirit Guardians range. The door might be too narrow for a horse — DM's call on whether it was open when they entered.
- The door they entered through is now magically sealed (Dispel Magic DC 15, or Strength DC 20 to break).

### Dynamic Brazier Mechanic

The braziers can be knocked over (Athletics DC 12). Each extinguished brazier has **two effects:**

**Ritual Effect:** Each brazier extinguished reduces the ritual's Charisma save DC by 2. At 3+ braziers out, the ritual fails entirely and Locke loses concentration on ongoing spells.

**Spell Immunity Effect:** Each brazier also reduces Locke's spell immunity by one level. The braziers are anchoring the ritual that empowers him — as they fall, his defenses crumble:

| Braziers Out | Spell Immunity | Ritual Save DC | What Unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (start) | Immune to 3rd level and below | DC 15 | Nothing — physical attacks only |
| 1 | Immune to 2nd level and below | DC 13 | **3rd-level spells work:** Counterspell, Fireball, Dispel Magic, Spirit Guardians, Hypnotic Pattern |
| 2 | Immune to 1st level and below | DC 11 | **2nd-level spells work:** Hold Person, Suggestion, Heat Metal, Silence, Invisibility |
| 3+ | Immune to cantrips only / **Ritual fails** | Ritual ends | **1st-level spells work:** Command, Tasha's Hideous Laughter. Locke loses concentration. |
| 4+ | **No spell immunity** | — | **Everything works.** Cantrips, Vicious Mockery, Chill Touch — full arsenal. |

**Why this works narratively:** The braziers aren't just ritual components — they're channels of fiendish power that sustain Locke's supernatural defenses. As the ritual crumbles, so does he. The party experiences a dramatic arc: spells bouncing off → scrambling for braziers → the moment Counterspell becomes available → unleashing everything at once.

**Aura of Protection vs. Ritual Saves:** With Jean's Aura active (+3), the ritual saves are fairly easy. Martin (+10 CHA save with Aura) auto-passes. Jean (+6) needs 9+. Lómë (+3) needs 12+. Oda (+3, but **Gnome Cunning grants advantage** on CHA saves vs. magic) has ~70% chance. If Jean is dominated and moves away, the Aura goes with him — suddenly everyone loses +3, making the ritual far more dangerous. This urgency to break the Dominate is intentional.

**Victory Conditions:**
- **Kill or incapacitate Locke.** He doesn't flee — his arrogance is his downfall.
- **Disrupt the ritual** (extinguish 3+ braziers) and escape through the door.
- **Both** — the ideal outcome.

### Path to the Grave + Divine Smite Combo

**Expect this.** Lómë uses Path to the Grave (Channel Divinity, action) to curse Locke with vulnerability to the next attack. Jean follows with a Divine Smite attack:
- Warhammer (1d8+3) + Smite (2d8) = avg ~16 damage, **doubled to ~32**
- This removes roughly 1/3 of Locke's 110 HP in one attack

**Timing matters:** Path to the Grave costs Lómë's action, so it happens on her turn. Jean acts on his own turn. By then, Locke has likely already cast Dominate Person on Round 1. If Jean is dominated, the combo falls apart — which makes the Dominate even more impactful. If Jean saved, this combo is devastating and should be rewarded.

**If Locke is defeated:**

> **Read Aloud:**
> *The Rakshasa crumples, that terrible smile still frozen on his face even as the light fades from his golden eyes. The braziers gutter and die. The circle's glow flickers once, twice, and goes dark. The warehouse is suddenly, oppressively silent.*
>
> *The puzzle box sits where Locke dropped it. Its geometric patterns have gone still — truly still, for the first time since you've held it. The shifting symbols that moved when you weren't looking are frozen in place, like a held breath.*
>
> *A fragment of the Pashupatastra. A weapon that could unmake the world. Still sealed. Still waiting.*

### Epilogue

The players now have choices. Let them discuss — there's no wrong answer:

- **Keep the box.** A fragment of a god-killing weapon is the most dangerous thing in Waterdeep. Who else knows about it? Locke didn't work alone — those Cult Fanatics came from somewhere.
- **Destroy it.** Arcana DC 20 reveals: the box can't be destroyed by mundane means. Veymar's journal (if they looted his study) suggests he spent months trying. A temple of Mystra or Helm might know how — but would they?
- **Return it to Lord Veymar.** He was trying to figure out how to destroy it. Maybe he's the right custodian — or maybe his obsession makes him the worst one.
- **Give it to a faction.** The Harpers, the Lords' Alliance, a temple. But can you trust any organization with the power to end the world?
- **Walk away.** Leave it in the warehouse. Not their problem anymore. But they'll always know it's out there.

> **Read Aloud:**
> *The night air of the Dock District hits you as you step outside. The salt, the brine, the distant creak of ships at anchor. Waterdeep goes on, oblivious to what almost happened in this warehouse.*
>
> *Somewhere out in the planes, the other fragments of the Pashupatastra still exist — sealed in their own boxes, protected by their own locks. Locke is dead, but he was just one Rakshasa. The weapon of the Destroyer was broken into pieces for a reason.*
>
> *Tonight, you kept one piece sealed. One lock held.*
>
> *For now.*

## DM Notes & Tips

### Pacing Guide
- If running long: Skip or shorten the intel-gathering phase (Act 2). Give them 1-2 freebies.
- If running short: Add a random encounter in the mansion (a drunk noble who won't stop talking, a servant who recognizes one of them).
- The finale should get at least 30 minutes. If you're at the 2-hour mark and they haven't escaped the mansion yet, let them get out clean and move to the drop-off.

### Loot
- **Locke's payment (if they find his stash):** 2,500 gp in a chest near the warehouse wall.

The dagger should also be visible on a crate during the fight (see Act 6 battlefield notes) — the Rogue needs it to deal damage through Locke's nonmagical immunity.
- **Veymar's study (if they looted):** A Scroll of Counterspell, a Potion of Greater Healing, and a leather-bound journal about the box (plot hook — Veymar discovered the box contains a fragment of the Pashupatastra and was desperately trying to find a way to destroy it. His final entry reads: *"If I cannot destroy it, I must ensure no one ever opens it. Five souls. That is the price. I pray no one is willing to pay it."*).

### Party Magic Weapon Checklist
Before the final fight, mentally track who can deal magical damage to Locke:
- **Jean:** Needs to cast **Magic Weapon** (2nd level, bonus action, concentration) on his warhammer/lance. This targets his weapon, not Locke, so spell immunity doesn't interfere. Alternatively, Divine Smite's radiant damage bypasses immunity (delivered via weapon, not a spell), but the weapon's base B/P/S damage is still blocked without Magic Weapon active.
- **Oda:** **Primal Strike** in beast form — all attacks magical. She's the most reliable damage source from Round 1.
- **Rogue:** Needs the **+1 ornate dagger** from the warehouse. Place it visibly on a crate. Three PCs have passive Perception 16 — they'll spot it.
- **Martin:** No weapon, no magical damage. Support role (Cutting Words, Inspiration, Healing Word, Dispel Magic on dominated allies). Once braziers fall, his spells come online.
- **Lómë:** Spiritual Weapon (force damage, targets Locke with a spell attack — but it's a 2nd-level spell, so it's blocked by immunity until 2 braziers are out). Mace is mundane. Her best offensive contribution early is **Path to the Grave** (vulnerability curse) setting up Jean or Oda for doubled damage.
- **Fallback:** If 3+ braziers are extinguished, Locke's nonmagical immunity also downgrades to **resistance** (as noted in stat-blocks.md). This is the ultimate safety valve.

## Appendix: The Pashupatastra — Lore Reference

The **Pashupatastra** is the most powerful weapon in Hindu mythology, wielded by Shiva (the Destroyer). In the Mahabharata, even the hero Arjuna — who was granted the weapon — was warned never to use it against mortals, for it would annihilate all creation indiscriminately.

In this adventure, the weapon was broken into fragments and sealed in boxes of blood magic by an ancient coalition of celestials. Each fragment requires five souls to unseal. Locke (the Rakshasa) sought this fragment to reassemble the weapon and use it to reshape the planes in his image — a being of pure ego wielding a weapon of pure annihilation.

**Campaign Hook:** If you ever want to continue this story, the other fragments are still out there. Other Rakshasa — perhaps Locke's kin — are searching for them. The signet ring on Locke's body bears the symbol of their network. The players may have stopped one heist, but the race for the Pashupatastra has only just begun.
