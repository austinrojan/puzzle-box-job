## Act 3 — Infiltration: Getting Inside (~20-30 min)

The gala is underway. Carriages line the circular drive. Music and light pour from the ballroom windows. The estate is alive with activity — perfect cover, or a den of watchful eyes.

> **Read Aloud:**
> *The Veymar estate rises from the Sea District like a monument to old money. Three stories of pale stone, ivy-laced walls, and tall arched windows glowing amber from within. A wrought-iron fence rings the property, its gate flanked by two guards in polished half-plate. Beyond, you can hear the murmur of a hundred conversations and the lilting notes of a string quartet.*

This party will almost certainly split. The natural breakdown: **Martin + Jean** as the social team (Martin's Entertainer background + Performance +7, Jean's Knight background + Persuasion +6 — avoid the Fake Invitation route, DC 16 Deception is brutal for them at +1 and +3). **Rogue + Oda** as the stealth team (Wild Shape + lockpicking, aim for the study). **Lómë** is the wildcard — she can join the social group as Jean's "spiritual advisor" (Knight households often have chaplains), take the sewer route (CON +2 handles DC 12), or disguise as a servant ("new temple hire" for the household chapel).

### Entry Methods

The party should choose **one approach** (though creative combinations are fine). Each entry has a primary check and a consequence for failure.

---

**1. Main Gate — Bluff or Force**

> *The main gate is a grand affair — twin panels of wrought iron, each bearing the Veymar family crest in hammered bronze. Beyond it, a cobblestone drive curves through manicured lawns toward the front entrance. A dozen carriages idle along the approach, their drivers sharing a flask and trading gossip. The two guards at the gate stand with the relaxed posture of men who've been checking invitations all evening and have grown bored of it — but their eyes are still sharp, and their halberds are not decorative.*

**DM Note:** The guards are professional but human. They've been standing here for three hours. One is younger and more by-the-book; the other is a veteran who just wants the night to end.

**NPC Dialogue — If the party approaches:**

> *The older guard holds up a hand. "Evening. Invitations, please." He says it the way a man says it for the fortieth time tonight — flat, rehearsed, already half-looking past you to the next carriage.*
>
> *The younger one straightens up. "Names on the list, ser. Lord Veymar's orders — no exceptions."*

- **Approach:** Talk their way past the guards or overpower them.
- **Check:** Deception DC 15 (claim to be guests, deliverymen, etc.) or initiative for combat (2 Guards, see stat blocks).

**Success:**

> *The older guard glances at his partner, shrugs, and steps aside. The iron gate swings open on oiled hinges, and the sounds of the gala wash over you — laughter, clinking glass, the swell of strings. The cobblestone drive stretches ahead, lined with flickering lanterns. You're in. Just like that. Nobody looks twice at people who walk like they belong.*

- Walk right in through the front door.

**Failure (Deception):**

> *The younger guard's eyes narrow. He takes a half-step forward, one hand drifting to the halberd. "I don't have that name on the list, ser. And I've been checking it all night." The older guard isn't smiling anymore either. He shakes his head slowly. "Best move along. Lord Veymar doesn't appreciate uninvited company."*

- Turned away. Must find another entry. +1 Heat.

**Failure (Combat):**

> *The halberd comes down with a crack against the cobblestones — not a swing, a signal. Before you can blink, a whistle shrieks from somewhere on the wall, and lanterns that were dim a moment ago flare to life along the perimeter. You hear boots on gravel — more guards, coming fast.*

- If they fight, it's loud. +2 Heat immediately (Alarmed).

---

**2. Servant's Entrance (East Side)**

> *The east side of the estate is quieter — no carriages, no lanterns, no guests. Just a narrow alley between the outer wall and a row of hedges, and at the far end, a plain wooden door set into the stone. The smell of roasting meat and baking bread drifts from a vent above the door — the kitchen is just on the other side. You can hear the muffled clatter of pots, the bark of a head cook giving orders, and someone laughing. A single guard paces a slow circuit along this stretch of wall, pausing every few minutes to lean against a buttress and pick at his fingernails.*

- **Requires:** Scouting intel or Pip's info (otherwise they don't know it exists — DC 16 Perception to find it blind).

**DM Note:** If the party bribed Pip, they know the door is often propped open during the busiest kitchen hours. Oda's Stealth +4 is borderline here — the Rogue should take point. If Oda wants to Wild Shape into a cat, that's smart: cats don't arouse suspicion near a kitchen. Let her bypass the Stealth check with a DC 12 Animal Handling (she has +6, needs a 6+) to act like a convincing stray.

**NPC Dialogue — If spotted by the guard:**

> *The guard straightens up, squinting into the dark. "Oi — who's there? Kitchen's closed to outside staff." He doesn't draw his sword, but his hand rests on the pommel. "If you're with the catering company, you're late. Use the front and talk to the steward."*

- **Check:** Stealth DC 13 (a single guard patrols this area) + Sleight of Hand DC 12 (the door is locked, simple lock).

**Success:**

> *The guard turns the corner, and you move. The lock yields with a soft click — a simple mechanism, barely worth the pick. The door swings inward on silent hinges, and a wall of heat and noise hits you: the roar of ovens, the clang of copper pans, steam rising from a dozen pots. Nobody looks up. Every servant in the kitchen is moving at a dead sprint — trays of glazed pheasant, towers of pastry, a boy hauling a cask of wine that weighs more than he does. You slip through the chaos like another pair of hands that nobody has time to question.*

- Slip in through the kitchen corridor.

**Failure:**

> *The guard's head snaps up. He drops the fingernail he was worrying at and takes three quick strides toward you, one hand on his sword. "You — stay right there!" His voice carries. From inside the kitchen, you hear the clatter of a dropped tray, and someone shouts, "What's going on out there?"*

- The guard calls for backup. +1 Heat.

---

**3. Scaling the Wall (West Side)**

> *The west side of the estate backs up against a row of townhouses, their upper windows dark. The stone wall here is old — older than the rest of the estate, probably part of the original property before the Veymars rebuilt. Rough-cut blocks, thick with ivy, the mortar crumbling in places. Twelve feet high, give or take. Beyond the wall, you can see the dark shapes of garden topiaries and hear the faint trickle of a fountain. Up on the roof of the estate's west wing, a figure leans against a chimney — an archer, silhouetted against the sky, a longbow slung across his back. He's watching the garden, but every few seconds his gaze sweeps the wall.*

**DM Note:** The Rogue is the natural pick here — Stealth expertise makes the DC 14 comfortable. Oda in spider form can bypass the Athletics check entirely (spider climb). If Jean tries to climb in his plate armor, he has disadvantage on the Athletics check — he's going to clank. Keep him away from this route.

- **Check:** Athletics DC 14 (the wall is 12 feet, rough stone) + Stealth DC 14 (a rooftop archer has line of sight).

**Success:**

> *Your fingers find the gaps in the mortar — one handhold, then two, then your foot catches on a ridge of ivy-thick stone and you pull yourself up. The wall bites cold into your palms. At the top, you flatten yourself against the capstones and wait. The archer on the roof turns, scans the far side of the garden, and you drop — silent, controlled — into a bed of soft earth and trimmed lavender. The scent rises around you, sharp and clean. Above, the archer turns back. He sees nothing. The garden stretches ahead, dark and inviting.*

- Over the wall, into the gardens.

**Failure (Athletics):**

> *Your fingers slip. The ivy tears free in a clump, and for a sickening moment you're hanging by one hand, feet scrabbling against smooth stone — and then you're falling. The ground meets you hard, knocking the wind from your lungs. You lie there for a moment, staring up at the wall that just beat you, the taste of dirt in your mouth.*

- Fall, take 1d6 bludgeoning, must retry or find another way.

**Failure (Stealth):**

> *You're halfway up when you hear it — the creak of a bow being drawn. The archer on the roof has turned, and he's looking right at you, framed against the wall like a fly on a tablecloth. He doesn't shoot. He doesn't have to. He puts two fingers to his lips and lets out a whistle that cuts through the garden like a knife.*

- The archer raises an alarm. +1 Heat.

**4. The Sewers (Beneath the Estate)**

> *The entrance to the sewer system is a rusted iron grate set into the cobblestones of a back alley, two blocks south of the estate. When you pull it open, the smell hits you first — wet stone, stagnant water, and something organic and deeply unpleasant that you don't want to think too hard about. A rusted ladder descends into darkness. Below, you can hear the echo of running water and the occasional skittering of things that live where the light doesn't reach. The tunnels are old — pre-Veymar, pre-Sea District, maybe pre-Waterdeep. They go everywhere. Including, if your sense of direction holds, directly beneath the estate.*

**DM Note:** Lómë is well-suited for this route — CON +2 handles the DC 12, Religion +6 might help her recognize old temple carvings in the tunnel walls (flavor only), and Darkvision means she doesn't need a torch. The Rogue can navigate (Survival or Thieves' Tools proficiency for reading the tunnel layout). If anyone with low CON takes this route, warn them: *"The smell gets worse the deeper you go. Much worse."*

- **Check:** Survival DC 13 (navigating the tunnels) + Constitution DC 12 (the smell is atrocious).

**NPC Encounter — Optional Flavor:** If you want to add tension, have the party encounter a half-mad sewer worker (not hostile, just startling) who mumbles directions before shuffling off into the dark:

> *A voice from the dark. "Left at the third fork. Don't take the right — that one floods." A figure shuffles past you in the ankle-deep water, carrying a hooded lantern that throws wild shadows on the walls. He doesn't stop, doesn't look at you. "And hold your breath past the old cistern. Trust me."*

**Success:**

> *The tunnels twist and branch, but you keep your bearings — left, then straight, then down a set of ancient stone steps that are slick with something you refuse to examine. The air gets colder, cleaner, and then you see it: a wooden trapdoor in the ceiling, light seeping through the cracks. You push it open and the smell of aged oak and fermented grapes floods your senses. Wine racks. Hundreds of bottles, resting in stone alcoves, stretching back into the dark. You're in the cellar. You're beneath the estate. And nobody knows you're here.*

- Emerge in the basement wine cellar. Bypasses all exterior security.

**Failure (Survival):**

> *Every tunnel looks the same — wet stone, brackish water, the same dripping echo from every direction. You double back twice, take a wrong turn at what might have been the third fork or the fourth, and waste precious time retracing your steps through water that's getting deeper. By the time you find the right trapdoor, you've lost almost an hour.*

- Lost for a while — lose time. No Heat, but one less round of exploration in the mansion.

**Failure (Constitution):**

> *The old cistern is the worst of it. The water here is black and still, and the stench that rises from it is something primal — your body rebels before your mind catches up. Your stomach heaves. Your eyes water. You press on, but the damage is done. The taste sits in the back of your throat, and every breath feels like swallowing something rotten. You can't shake it.*

- Poisoned condition for 1 hour (disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks). Nasty.

**5. Hidden Garden Passage (Conservatory)**

> *The conservatory is a beautiful structure — glass walls and an iron frame, glowing faintly from the phosphorescent orchids within. It sits at the edge of the formal gardens, half-hidden by the hedge maze that sprawls out from its entrance. The air here smells of wet earth and night-blooming jasmine. From somewhere inside the maze, you hear the low, guttural sound of a dog panting — then a second one, deeper. The mastiffs. They're close.*

- **Requires:** Groundskeeper intel (otherwise unknown).

**DM Note:** This is Oda's route. Animal Handling +6 vs DC 15 means she needs a 9+ — doable but not guaranteed. If she Wild Shapes into a dog, give her advantage on the check (pack behavior). If the Rogue is with her, they can take the Stealth option while Oda handles the dogs. Splitting the challenge between two characters is smart — reward it.

**NPC Encounter — The Mastiffs:**

> *Two shapes materialize from the hedge maze — massive dogs, barrel-chested, with broad skulls and cropped ears. They don't bark. They don't growl. They just stop, twenty feet away, and stare at you with the flat, unblinking focus of animals that have been trained to decide very quickly whether you're a threat. One of them lowers its head. The muscles in its shoulders bunch.*

- **Check:** Stealth DC 14 (hedge maze has 2 Mastiffs patrolling — Animal Handling DC 15 to calm them, or Stealth to avoid).

**Success (Animal Handling):**

> *You lower yourself slowly — no sudden movements — and extend a hand, palm down. The lead mastiff approaches, stiff-legged, ears flat. It sniffs your hand. Its nostrils flare. For a long, taut moment, nothing happens. Then the tail moves — just once, a slow wag. The second dog follows its partner's lead, and within a minute they're both pressing their heads against your legs, tongues lolling, the threat forgotten. You scratch behind an ear that's bigger than your hand, and the mastiff's whole body relaxes. Beyond them, the conservatory door is unguarded.*

**Success (Stealth):**

> *You press yourself into the hedge wall and hold your breath. One of the mastiffs passes within arm's reach — close enough to feel the heat of its body, to hear the wet rhythm of its breathing. It pauses. Sniffs the air. Your heart hammers against your ribs. Then it moves on, following some scent trail deeper into the maze. You slip through the gap it left and reach the conservatory door without a sound.*

- Enter through the conservatory. Inside, behind the big fern with red leaves — the stone trapdoor, just like the groundskeeper described. It opens onto a set of narrow stone stairs leading down to the wine cellar.

**Failure:**

> *The lead mastiff throws its head back and lets out a bark that echoes off the estate walls like a thunderclap. The second joins in — a deep, booming alarm that shreds the quiet of the garden. Lights flare in the guard house. You hear voices, boots on gravel, the metallic ring of weapons being drawn. The dogs don't chase you. They don't have to. They've done their job.*

- Dogs bark. +1 Heat. Guards investigate the conservatory.

**6. Fake Invitation (Front Door)**

> *The front entrance is a spectacle in itself. A red carpet has been laid down the front steps, flanked by enchanted lanterns that shift through jewel tones — emerald, sapphire, ruby. Guests arrive in pairs and small groups, the women in silk and brocade, the men in velvet doublets with silver buttons. A steward in a pressed black coat stands at the door, checking invitations against a leather-bound ledger with the pinched efficiency of a man who memorized the guest list three days ago. His quill scratches, his eyes flick up, his smile is practiced and empty. Beyond him, through the open doors, you can see the ballroom — a cathedral of candlelight, with a crystal chandelier the size of a carriage throwing prismatic light across a hundred upturned faces.*

**DM Note:** This is the hardest social route. DC 16 Deception to forge or present a fake invitation is brutal for this party — their best Deception is Jean at +3. Only recommend this if the Rogue has Deception proficiency or the party acquired actual invitations during Act 2. If they insist, Martin can use Performance DC 14 as a secondary check to "sell the act," which is more in his wheelhouse (+7). But honestly, steer them toward Entry 7 (Performer's Entrance) if they want the front door — it's built for Martin.

- **Check:** Forgery/Deception DC 16 (create or acquire a convincing invitation) + Performance or Deception DC 14 (act the part of a noble).

**NPC Dialogue — The Steward:**

> *The steward's smile doesn't waver, but his eyes do the work — they drop to your clothes, your shoes, the quality of the parchment you're holding, all in the space of a breath.*
>
> *"Good evening. Your invitation, if you please." He extends a gloved hand. His other hand hovers near the ledger, quill poised.*

**Success:**

> *The steward examines the invitation, glances at his ledger, and makes a small, precise checkmark. "Very good, my lord. Welcome to the Veymar estate." He steps aside with a practiced bow, and the doors are open. The ballroom swallows you in warmth and light — the perfume of a hundred nobles, the shimmer of silk, the cascade of a harp being played in the far corner. A servant materializes at your elbow with a silver tray of champagne flutes. You take one. You belong here. At least, tonight you do.*

- Walk in as a guest. Full access to the ballroom and ground floor. Best social infiltration route.

**Failure:**

> *The steward's quill stops. He looks up from the ledger, and the practiced smile is gone, replaced by the particular expression of a man who has found a discrepancy.*
>
> *"I'm terribly sorry, but I don't seem to have this name on tonight's list. One moment." He raises a hand, and you see a guard in polished half-plate approaching from the foyer. "Captain Helm will want to have a brief word. I'm sure it's just a misunderstanding."*

- Captain Helm is called. Questioned and escorted out. +1 Heat. Can still re-enter another way.

**7. Performer's Entrance (Party-Specific — Martin)**

> *The side entrance near the ballroom's service corridor is a controlled chaos of its own — musicians tuning instruments, a fire-eater practicing in the alley, two acrobats stretching against the wall, and a flustered woman with a clipboard pacing back and forth, her hair escaping its pins. This is where the entertainment checks in. The smell of rosin, sweat, and stage paint hangs in the air. A hand-painted sign on an easel reads: "PERFORMERS — CHECK IN WITH MADAME LYSARA. NO EXCEPTIONS. NO WALK-INS. I MEAN IT."*

- **Approach:** Martin uses his Entertainer background to talk his way in as the evening's musical act (or a last-minute addition to the entertainment lineup).

**DM Note:** This is Martin's moment. Performance +7 against DC 14 means he needs a 7 — he'll almost certainly nail it. Let Robben describe his character's pitch. If Martin offers to audition on the spot, drop the Performance DC to 12 (let him show off — that's what Bards are for). The Deception DC 12 secondary check is low intentionally — performing *is* Martin's truth. He's not lying about being an entertainer. He's barely even stretching.

**NPC Dialogue — Madame Lysara (Event Coordinator):**

> *The woman with the clipboard looks up as Martin approaches. She has the harried energy of someone who has dealt with six emergencies in the last hour and is bracing for the seventh.*
>
> *"Are you on my list? Please tell me you're on my list." She flips through pages without waiting for an answer. "I had a lutist cancel — food poisoning, he says, though I suspect cold feet — and the contortionist is drunk, and Lord Veymar specifically requested a vocalist for the midnight set, which I do not have, because apparently nobody in this city can carry a tune and also show up on time."*
>
> *She stops flipping and looks at Martin — really looks at him, for the first time.*
>
> *"Wait. Are you a musician? Please, for the love of all that is holy, tell me you are a musician."*

**If Martin says yes (or performs a sample):**

> *Lysara's eyes widen. She grabs Martin by the arm — not gently — and starts pulling him toward the service entrance.*
>
> *"You're hired. I don't care what your name is. I don't care where you came from. Can you play for two hours? Can you do requests? Never mind, it doesn't matter. You're on at eleven. The green room is through that door, second left, don't touch the wine — that's for the guests. Do you have a page-turner? An assistant? Bring them, I don't care, just be ready at eleven."*

- **Check:** Performance DC 14 (convince the event coordinator or the guards he's legitimate) + Deception DC 12 (sell the story if questioned further — low DC because performing *is* his truth).

**Success:**

> *Martin walks through the service entrance like he's done it a hundred times before — because he has, at a hundred other venues. The corridor smells of candle wax and polished wood. A stagehand nods to him, another hands him a glass of water without being asked. This is backstage. This is his world. He knows the rhythm of it — the nervous energy, the last-minute tuning, the hush before the curtain. Through a gap in the stage drapes, he can see the ballroom: packed, glittering, expectant. They don't know it yet, but the best performer of the evening just walked in through the back door.*

- Martin enters through the main entrance with his instruments, given access to the ballroom and servants' areas. He can bring one companion as his "assistant" (instrument carrier, page-turner, etc.).

**Failure:**

> *Lysara squints at her clipboard, then at Martin, then back at the clipboard. She shakes her head.*
>
> *"I'm sorry, love. I appreciate the initiative, truly, but we've got a full program tonight and Lord Veymar approved the lineup personally. I can't just add people." She pats his arm with genuine sympathy. "Try the Moonstone Mask — they're always looking for talent. Tell them Lysara sent you."*

- Turned away — "We already have a full program tonight." No Heat (it's a reasonable approach, just bad luck). Can try another method.

**Why this exists:** Martin has Performance +7 but Deception +1. This route plays to his actual strengths.

### Transition — You're Inside. Now What?

Once the party is inside — whether through one entrance or split across several — read the appropriate passage based on where they entered:

**If they entered through the front (Main Gate, Fake Invitation, or Performer's Entrance):**

> *The warmth of the ballroom envelops you. Candlelight catches on crystal and silver. The string quartet has shifted into something slow and stately, and a hundred conversations blend into a single, thrumming hum. Servants weave between guests with trays of champagne and canapés. No one is looking at you. Everyone is looking at everyone else. Somewhere above you — up past the grand staircase, past the second-floor gallery, behind a locked door on the third floor — is a puzzle box that someone is paying you a lot of gold to steal.*
>
> *The clock in the foyer reads half past nine. Dawn is a long way off. But the night isn't going to wait for you.*

**If they entered through the kitchen or servant's entrance:**

> *The kitchen corridor is narrow, hot, and loud. Steam hisses from somewhere, and a cook shouts an order that nobody acknowledges. You press against the wall as a servant rushes past with a tureen of soup, and then you're through — into a quieter hallway, dimly lit, with a servants' staircase rising to the left and the distant sound of music drifting from the ballroom to the right. You are backstage in someone else's house. Every door you open from here is a gamble.*

**If they entered through the wine cellar (Sewers or Conservatory Passage):**

> *The wine cellar is cold and silent after the chaos of getting here. Racks of dusty bottles stretch into the dark, and the stone floor is dry and clean. A single staircase leads up — narrow, wooden, the steps worn smooth by decades of servants hauling bottles to dinner parties. At the top, you can see a sliver of light beneath a closed door. The muffled sounds of the gala filter down: music, laughter, the clink of glass. You're beneath the heart of the estate. Everything is above you now.*

**If they entered through the gardens (Scaling the Wall):**

> *The garden is darker than you expected. Topiaries loom like sentinels, and the fountain gurgles somewhere to your left. The ballroom windows blaze with light on the far side of the grounds — you can see figures moving behind the glass, silhouettes dancing. Between you and the house: fifty yards of open lawn, a gravel path that will crunch underfoot, and the occasional sweep of a lantern from the upper windows. The back door is there. You just have to reach it.*

**DM Note:** If the party split up to use different entrances, this is the moment to reunite them. Ask each group: *"Where are you heading?"* The natural convergence point is the **ground floor main hallway** — it connects the kitchen, the ballroom, and the main foyer. Or, if one group went through the cellar and another through the front, have them run into each other on the servants' staircase for a tense, funny moment of mutual recognition. Once everyone is inside and oriented, move to Act 4.
