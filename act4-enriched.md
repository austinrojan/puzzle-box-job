## Act 4 — Navigating the Mansion (~20-30 min)

They're inside. Now they need to reach the **third-floor study**. The mansion layout:

### Mansion Layout (Simplified)

```
GROUND FLOOR          SECOND FLOOR         THIRD FLOOR
─────────────         ─────────────        ─────────────
Ballroom (west)       Guest Rooms          Veymar's Study ★
Dining Hall           Library              Private Gallery
Kitchen / Servants'   Lady Thessaly's      Arcane Workshop
  Quarters              Parlor
Main Foyer            Upper Landing        Locked Stairwell
Wine Cellar (below)                          (from 2nd floor)
```

The **stairwell to the third floor is locked** (Thieves' Tools DC 15 or the key, which Captain Helm carries). There's also a balcony on the third floor accessible from outside (Athletics DC 16 to climb up).

### Setting the Tone

> *The inside of the Veymar estate hits you like stepping into another world. The ground floor hums with life — warm candlelight reflected in polished marble, the low murmur of a hundred overlapping conversations, the clink of crystal, the perfume of hothouse flowers competing with roasted meat and mulled wine. Somewhere deeper in the house, the string quartet shifts into a waltz, and the crowd responds like a living thing — bodies turning, dresses swirling, laughter rising and falling like waves.*
>
> *But beneath all of it — beneath the music and the chatter and the warm golden light — there's the constant, quiet movement of people who are watching. A guard in polished half-plate stationed by the main staircase. A servant whose eyes linger a half-second too long. The flash of a captain's insignia disappearing around a corner.*
>
> *This place is beautiful. It is also a cage with very attentive keepers.*

### Three Paths to the Study

**Jean's Passive Perception is 9.** He will walk past traps, wards, and clues without noticing. The other three (Martin, Lómë, Oda at passive 16) need to spot things and share info. This creates natural party dynamics — the observant characters guide the confident but oblivious paladin.

Players will likely combine approaches. Present these as the natural options based on where they entered:

---

**Path A — Through the Ballroom (Social)**

Mingle with guests, gather info, work their way upstairs.

> *The ballroom is a cathedral of vanity. Three crystal chandeliers hang from a vaulted ceiling painted with scenes of Waterdhavian conquest — ships, dragons, men in armor striking heroic poses. Below them, a sea of silk and velvet swirls across a floor of black-and-white marble tile. The string quartet plays on a raised platform near the west windows, framed by curtains of deep crimson. Servants weave through the crowd carrying silver trays of wine and tiny, architectural food — the kind that looks like a jewel and tastes like a promise.*
>
> *At the far end of the ballroom, a grand staircase sweeps upward to the second floor. A single guard stands at the bottom, hands clasped behind his back, watching the crowd with the bored patience of a man who does this every week.*

**Checks:** Deception or Performance DC 13 (blend in), Persuasion DC 14 (get a noble to vouch for them or distract guards), Stealth DC 14 (slip upstairs unnoticed during a toast or performance).

**DM Note:** Martin has +7 Performance — this is his room. If the party entered via the Performer's Entrance, he's already got a reason to be on the stage. Let him own this. Jean's Knight background means he knows how to hold a wine glass and nod at the right moments, even if his Passive Perception means he's oblivious to the guard counting heads.

**The Nosy Noble — Lord Pemberton:**

Within a few minutes of mingling, a noble approaches whoever looks most out of place (probably Lómë or the Rogue, unless they're well-disguised).

> *A portly man in a burgundy doublet waddles over with the unstoppable momentum of someone who has never been told to go away. His face is flushed — he's at least four glasses of wine into the evening — and his smile is the kind that expects you to be delighted by his attention.*
>
> *"I say! I don't believe I've had the pleasure. Lord Desmond Pemberton. I know everyone, of course — it's rather my thing — and I'm quite certain I don't know you. Which means you must be terribly interesting, or terribly lost."*
>
> *He laughs at his own joke. It goes on too long.*

**DM Note:** Pemberton is harmless but loud. He's a social obstacle, not a threat. The danger is that he draws attention and won't shut up. A Persuasion DC 12 redirects him ("Lord Pemberton, is that Lady Gralhund by the punch bowl? I believe she was looking for you."). A Performance DC 11 entertains him enough that he becomes an unwitting ally ("You simply MUST meet Captain Helm — she's always so serious, she needs someone like you to liven things up!" — this is a trap; avoid it). Ignoring him or being rude doesn't raise Heat, but he'll loudly complain about the "odd newcomers" within earshot of a guard.

**Lady Thessaly — The Tipsy Socialite:**

If players reach the area near the grand staircase, or if they're looking for an ally, Lady Thessaly finds them. She's been drinking steadily since the gala began.

> *A woman in an emerald gown detaches herself from a cluster of laughing nobles and drifts toward you. She's striking — high cheekbones, dark hair pinned with silver combs, the kind of beauty that's been sharpened by years of smiling at people she despises. She holds her wine glass like a weapon.*
>
> *"You look like you're actually interesting. Do you know how rare that is at one of these things?"*
>
> *She takes a long sip of wine and looks you over with an appraiser's eye.*
>
> *"I'm Thessaly. Lady Thessaly, technically, though the 'Lady' part is mostly a formality at this point. Aldric — Lord Veymar, my husband — is upstairs doing whatever it is he does when he disappears for hours. Probably talking to that box again."*
>
> *She says this lightly, but her jaw tightens.*
>
> *"He used to talk to me. Now it's the box. Always the box. Tell me — do you find it normal for a grown man to whisper to an object?"*

**DM Note:** Thessaly is bitter, lonely, and three glasses deep. She's not an enemy — she genuinely resents Veymar's obsession. If treated with sympathy (Persuasion DC 12 or just good RP), she'll share useful info: the study is on the third floor, Veymar locks himself in every evening, the stairwell is locked, Captain Helm carries the only spare key. She'll even create a distraction if asked — but not consciously help them steal from her husband. Frame it as: "She might drunkenly cause a scene near the staircase guard if someone suggests her husband is ignoring her at his own party." No check required — just say the right thing and she'll do the rest.

If Lómë or Jean approach with genuine compassion (not manipulation), Thessaly opens up more:

> *She stares into her wine for a moment.*
>
> *"He wasn't always like this, you know. Before the box, he was just... eccentric. A collector. Harmless. But ever since that shipwreck haul three months ago — he doesn't sleep. He barely eats. His eyes have this..." She trails off. "Never mind. You didn't come here to listen to a wife complain."*
>
> *She straightens, and the socialite mask snaps back into place.*
>
> *"More wine?"*

**Encounter (Heat 1+): Captain Helm Approaches**

> *A woman in polished armor cuts through the crowd with the ease of someone who owns the room. She stops directly in front of you. Sharp eyes, close-cropped hair, a jaw that looks like it was carved from the same stone as the estate.*
>
> *"Good evening. Captain Dara Helm, head of Lord Veymar's security. I don't believe we've met."*
>
> *She doesn't extend a hand. She doesn't smile.*
>
> *"I make it a point to know every face on the guest list. And yours... I don't recall. Perhaps you could remind me — who extended your invitation?"*

Deception DC 16 to satisfy her — **this is a real threat for this party** (Martin has only +1 Deception, Jean has +3). Allow creative skill substitutions if the RP justifies it: Martin could try Performance +7 ("I'm the musical act, darling — do you need to see my set list?"), Persuasion +5 ("Captain, there seems to be a commotion near the east entrance..."), or Jean could try Intimidation +6 ("Do you know who I am?") or Persuasion +6 with his Knight bearing. If Jean uses **Conquering Presence** (Channel Divinity — frightened, WIS DC 14) on Helm as a last resort, it's dramatic and messy (+1 Heat minimum) but unforgettable RP.

**Success:**

> *Helm studies you for a long, uncomfortable moment. You can feel her measuring every word you said against whatever mental ledger she keeps. Then she exhales — not quite a sigh, not quite a dismissal.*
>
> *"Very well. Enjoy the evening."*
>
> *She turns on her heel and walks away. But you notice she doesn't go far — she takes up a position by the west pillar, where she can see the staircase and the ballroom at once. Her eyes find you twice more in the next five minutes.*

**Failure:** She doesn't arrest them, but she shadows the party for the rest of the act — all further Stealth checks in the mansion are made at disadvantage.

> *Helm's expression doesn't change. But something behind her eyes shifts — the way a hawk's pupils adjust before it dives.*
>
> *"Interesting. I'll be keeping an eye on you this evening. I'm sure you understand."*
>
> *She steps back but doesn't leave. For the rest of the night, every time you glance over your shoulder, she's there — by a pillar, at the edge of a doorway, her arms folded, watching.*

**The Near-Miss — Slipping Upstairs:**

When the party tries to reach the second floor via the grand staircase, build the tension:

> *The guard at the base of the staircase shifts his weight and cracks his neck. A servant passes between you and him, carrying a tower of champagne glasses. For three seconds — maybe four — his view of the staircase is completely blocked.*
>
> *This is the window.*

Stealth DC 14 to slip past. On success:

> *You move. The carpet on the stairs swallows your footsteps. The music swells — a crescendo, perfectly timed, as if the quartet were in on it. By the time the servant passes and the guard's eyes sweep back to the staircase, you're already rounding the banister on the second-floor landing, heart hammering, invisible from below.*

**Encounter (Heat 2):** Helm has posted a guard at the second-floor landing. Must be dealt with (Stealth DC 16 to sneak past, or combat — 1 Guard).

> *You reach the top of the stairs and your stomach drops. A second guard stands at the landing — arms folded, back against the wall, positioned to see anyone coming up from either staircase. He wasn't here before. Someone gave the order.*

---

**Path B — Through the Servants' Quarters (Stealth)**

Use back corridors, the dumbwaiter, and servant passages.

> *The servants' quarters are a different world from the ballroom — narrow corridors of bare stone and whitewashed plaster, lit by tallow candles that gutter in the drafts. The air smells of lye soap, boiled linen, and the ever-present undercurrent of roasted meat drifting from the kitchens. You can hear the distant music of the gala through the walls, muffled and strange, like a memory of a party you weren't invited to.*
>
> *Servants move through these corridors with the practiced speed of people who know they're invisible — heads down, trays balanced, voices low. Nobody looks at you. Nobody looks at each other, either.*

**Checks:** Stealth DC 13 (avoid servants), Investigation DC 12 (find the dumbwaiter — automatic if Pip told them).

**DM Note:** Oda's Wild Shape is ideal for scouting these corridors. A spider on the ceiling or a cat slinking through the shadows draws no suspicion from busy servants. If she scouts ahead, give the party advantage on their first Stealth check down here.

**A Close Call — The Busy Corridor:**

> *You're halfway down the servants' corridor when a door flies open six feet ahead of you. A young woman in a flour-dusted apron backs through it carrying a massive pot of something steaming. She kicks the door shut with her heel, turns, and walks directly toward you — head down, muttering a count under her breath.*
>
> *"...forty-two, forty-three, forty-four — who orders forty-four crab puffs, honestly..."*
>
> *She passes close enough that you can feel the heat rising from the pot. She doesn't look up. Her shoes squeak on the stone. She rounds the corner and is gone.*

No check required — this is a tension beat. Describe how the party holds their breath. If they're being loud or careless, make it a Stealth DC 10 (trivial, but the dice roll adds drama).

**Encounter (Heat 1+): The Cook Who Spots Them**

> *The kitchen is a furnace of organized chaos — three hearths blazing, copper pots clanging, the sizzle of fat and the bark of orders. You're trying to pass through quickly when a broad-shouldered woman in a grease-stained apron wheels around from the main stove and freezes. She's holding a cleaver in one hand and a half-butchered duck in the other. Her eyes narrow.*
>
> *"Oi. You."*
>
> *She points the cleaver at you. Not threateningly — more like a teacher who's caught someone in the wrong classroom.*
>
> *"I know every face that works this kitchen, and yours isn't one of them. You new? Because if you're new, nobody told me, and if nobody told me, you're not supposed to be here."*

Deception DC 14 ("New hire") or Intimidation DC 15 (scare them quiet).

**Success (Deception):**

> *She squints at you for a long moment, then grunts.*
>
> *"New hire. Of course. Nobody tells me anything. Lord's paying for a forty-plate dinner and they send me one warm body at the last minute with no training." She shoves a tray of bread rolls into your hands. "Make yourself useful. Take those to the dining hall. And if you drop a single one, you're scrubbing pots until dawn."*
>
> *She's already turned back to the stove before you can respond. The tray gives you perfect cover to cross the kitchen.*

**Success (Intimidation):**

> *She takes a half-step back, and the cleaver lowers an inch. Her mouth opens, closes. She glances at the door to the corridor, then back at you.*
>
> *"Right. None of my business. I didn't see anything." She turns back to her stove with exaggerated focus. "Just... don't touch the wine. He counts the bottles."*

**Failure:** She shouts for a guard. +1 Heat. The party has one round to leave the kitchen before a guard arrives.

**The Dumbwaiter:**

> *You find it behind a stack of flour sacks in the back pantry — a wooden shaft barely two feet square, with a hand-crank mechanism and a frayed rope that disappears into the darkness above. The wood is old, the pulleys are rusty, and when you look up the shaft, you can see the faint outline of the second-floor hatch ten feet above you.*
>
> *Something scurries in the darkness up there. Probably a rat. Hopefully a rat.*

The dumbwaiter goes to the **second floor only** — they still need to get to the third. It can carry one Small creature comfortably or one Medium creature uncomfortably (Acrobatics DC 10 to squeeze in). The crank is loud — Stealth DC 13 while operating it, or stuff cloth around the pulleys to muffle it (no check, but takes an extra minute).

**Encounter (Heat 2):** Servants have been told to watch for intruders. All Stealth DCs +2.

> *Something has changed. The servants are moving differently — checking over their shoulders, whispering in pairs, glancing at faces instead of floors. A steward you haven't seen before is walking the corridor with a lantern, opening doors and peering into rooms.*
>
> *Someone gave the order. They're looking for you.*

**A Close Call — The Nervous Servant:**

On the second floor, as the party navigates toward the third-floor stairwell:

> *You flatten against the wall as footsteps approach — quick, nervous steps, someone trying to be thorough rather than quiet. A young man in house livery appears at the end of the corridor, carrying a ring of keys. He stops at each door, unlocks it, peers inside for exactly three seconds, then relocks it and moves on.*
>
> *He's two doors away from you. One door. He reaches for the handle of the room you're standing next to.*
>
> *Then — from downstairs — a crash. Glass breaking. Someone in the ballroom shouts, and then laughter erupts. The servant flinches, looks toward the staircase, and mutters: "Lord save me, if that's another chandelier..."*
>
> *He pockets the keys and hurries toward the noise. The corridor is empty again.*

**DM Note:** This is a gift — let it happen naturally. If the players set up a distraction earlier (Lady Thessaly, Martin performing, etc.), credit their foresight. If not, it's just lucky timing. Either way, the tension of the approaching footsteps followed by the relief of the crash gives the players a visceral sense that this heist is alive and unpredictable.

**Path C — Outside Route (Acrobatic)**

Scale balconies and rooftops to reach the third-floor balcony directly.

> *The night air hits you like cold water after the warmth of the estate. Above, a half-moon hangs in a sky streaked with thin clouds. Below, the gardens are dark — hedge rows and gravel paths silvered with moonlight. Somewhere down there, a mastiff whines and is shushed by its handler.*
>
> *The second-floor balcony is twelve feet above you — weathered stone with iron railings, the kind of balcony that exists so wealthy people can stare at their own gardens. Beyond it, the rooftop stretches toward the third floor like a tilted field of dark slate tiles. From up here, you can see the third-floor balcony — a narrow ledge of stone railing and a glass door, maybe forty feet along the roofline.*
>
> *The wind picks up. A tile somewhere above shifts and clicks against its neighbor.*

**Checks:** Athletics DC 14 (climb to second-floor balcony), Acrobatics DC 15 (traverse rooftop to third-floor balcony), Stealth DC 14 (avoid the rooftop archer).

**The Climb:**

On success (Athletics):

> *Your fingers find the cracks between the stones — old mortar, crumbling in places, solid enough in others. You pull yourself up hand over hand, boots scraping against ivy that tears away in strips beneath your weight. For one bad moment, your foot slips and you swing by your fingers, dangling twelve feet above the gravel path. Then your hand finds the iron railing and you haul yourself over, landing on the balcony in a crouch.*
>
> *Below, a guard walks past on the garden path. He doesn't look up. People never look up.*

On failure (Athletics): Fall, take 1d6 bludgeoning, must retry or find another way. The noise may attract attention (Stealth DC 12 to stay quiet on impact, or +1 Heat).

**The Rooftop Crossing:**

> *The rooftop is a nightmare of angles. Slate tiles slick with evening dew, pitched at a grade steep enough that every step wants to become a slide. The wind is stronger up here — it tugs at your clothes, pushes at your balance, carries the muffled sound of the gala below like music from another life. The third-floor balcony waits at the far end, a dark rectangle against the pale stone.*
>
> *Between you and it: forty feet of tilted roof, a chimney stack, and the certain knowledge that a fall from this height will be loud, painful, and extremely difficult to explain.*

On success (Acrobatics):

> *You move in a low crouch, weight on your toes, fingers trailing against the tiles for balance. Each step is deliberate — test the tile, shift your weight, move. The chimney stack gives you a handhold at the halfway point, and you press your back against the warm brick and breathe. The city stretches below you — the Sea District's lamplit streets, the distant dark smudge of the harbor, the faint sound of the gala drifting up from below like a dream.*
>
> *Then you push off and cover the last twenty feet to the third-floor balcony. Your hands close around the stone railing and you pull yourself over, landing softly on the narrow ledge. The glass door is right there — dark, quiet, waiting.*

**Encounter (Heat 1+): The Rooftop Archer**

> *You hear him before you see him — a low, tuneless humming drifting from the east tower platform, twenty feet above and to your right. A man in leather armor sits on the tower's parapet, crossbow across his knees, staring out at the harbor. He's not paying attention. He's counting stars, or thinking about his dinner, or whatever bored sentries think about on nights when nothing ever happens.*
>
> *Then he shifts, stretching his neck, and his gaze begins to sweep the roofline.*

Must be avoided (Stealth DC 16) or neutralized quietly (melee attack, grapple, Sleep spell, etc.).

**DM Note:** If Oda Wild Shapes into a cat or owl and crosses the rooftop, the archer won't think twice — animals on the roof are normal. She can potentially scout the route or even distract the archer while others cross. If the Rogue is on this path, Stealth expertise should make DC 16 manageable. The real tension is anyone with lower Stealth (Jean at +0 should NOT be on this path — but if he insists, make the dice roll dramatic).

The archer, if the party observes him for a moment:

> *He lifts a wineskin, takes a pull, and mutters to himself.*
>
> *"'Watch the roof,' she says. 'Possible threat from above,' she says. What's going to come from above? A very ambitious seagull?"*
>
> *He takes another drink and goes back to staring at the harbor.*

**Encounter (Heat 2):** The rooftop is magically lit. Stealth checks made at disadvantage unless they dispel or destroy the light source.

> *The rooftop floods with cold blue light. Arcane lanterns — four of them, mounted at the corners of the roof ridge — flare to life simultaneously, turning the dark slate into a stage. Every shadow vanishes. Every tile is visible. You feel as exposed as an insect on a dinner plate.*
>
> *From the east tower, you hear the archer stand up. He's not humming anymore.*

The light source can be destroyed (AC 10, 5 HP each, but shattering one is audible), dispelled (Dispel Magic, DC 13 — Martin auto-succeeds), or avoided by timing movement between the lanterns' positions (Stealth DC 18 — the light is thorough but the chimney stack creates a narrow shadow corridor).

### The Third-Floor Stairwell Door

**Note on Jean's weapons:** His Warhorse and Mounted Combatant feat are useless inside the mansion. His lance has disadvantage within 5 ft without a mount. Inside, Jean fights with his **warhammer** (+6 to hit, 1d8+3 bludgeoning, Push mastery). Save the mount for the warehouse fight in Act 6 (60 ft x 40 ft — tight but possible).

However they get to the third floor, they face the **locked stairwell door** (unless they came via the outside balcony route, which bypasses it).

> *The second-floor corridor narrows as you move away from the guest rooms and toward the back of the house. The carpet thins, then disappears entirely. The wallpaper changes — from silk damask to bare plaster. The gala is a distant murmur now, two floors and a world away.*
>
> *At the end of the corridor, set into an alcove lit by a single guttering candle, is a heavy oak door reinforced with iron bands. A brass lock — newer than the door, recently installed — gleams in the candlelight. Beyond this door, a narrow staircase leads up to the third floor.*
>
> *From somewhere below, you hear footsteps. A patrol, maybe. Or a servant. Or nothing. But the sound reminds you: you're standing in the open, in a corridor with one way in and one way out, trying to open a door that someone very much does not want you to open.*

- **Thieves' Tools DC 15** to pick the lock.
- **Knock** or **Dispel Magic** opens it automatically (but Knock is audible within 300 ft — it will be heard. Instant +1 Heat).
- **Captain Helm's key** (pickpocketed: Sleight of Hand DC 17, or looted if she was knocked out).
- **Strength DC 20** to force it (loud — +1 Heat).

**DM Note:** The Rogue with Thieves' Tools expertise likely has +9 or more — DC 15 is manageable but not guaranteed. If they bought Knuckles' masterwork tools (+2), this becomes almost trivial. If they don't have the Rogue here (bad party split), this is a real problem. Martin's Knock spell works but announces their presence to every guard within 300 feet. Build the tension — describe the sound of those footsteps below getting closer while the lock is being worked.

**Picking the Lock — The Tension Beat:**

> *The pick slides into the lock. The first tumbler clicks — loud in the silence — and you freeze. Nothing. No shout, no footsteps. You exhale and go back to work.*
>
> *The second tumbler resists. You can feel it catching on something — a false groove, the kind a locksmith adds to frustrate exactly this kind of thing. The footsteps below are getting louder. Someone is coming up the main staircase.*
>
> *The third tumbler. Your fingers are steady but your pulse isn't. The footsteps reach the second-floor landing. A voice — a guard, calling to someone: "Anything up here?"*
>
> *Click.*

On success:

> *The lock turns. The door swings inward on oiled hinges — someone keeps this door well-maintained, even if they never want it opened. Beyond, a narrow staircase spirals upward into darkness.*
>
> *Behind you, the guard's voice again, closer: "No? Right. Check the library." Footsteps recede. You slip through the door and pull it shut behind you.*
>
> *You're through.*

On failure (Thieves' Tools): The lock doesn't budge. They can retry (takes another minute — roll again, but the patrol is closer) or find another way. Each retry increases the chance of discovery — after two failures, a guard rounds the corner unless the party has set up a distraction.

### Transition to the Third Floor

Whether they came through the stairwell door or the outside balcony, read this when they reach the third floor:

> *The third floor is different.*
>
> *You feel it the moment you step off the stairs — or over the balcony railing — and your feet touch the floorboards. The air is cooler here, and still, as if the gala below is happening in a different building entirely. The hallway is narrow, lit by a single arcane sconce that burns with a pale, steady blue flame that doesn't flicker. No drafts. No sound. The walls are bare stone — no wallpaper, no paintings, no pretense of domesticity. This is not a place where guests are welcomed.*
>
> *The floorboards are dark hardwood, old and smooth, and they creak faintly under your weight. Three doors line the hallway: one to the left (the private gallery), one to the right (the arcane workshop), and one at the far end, directly ahead — a heavy oak door with iron fittings and, you notice, faint lines of light tracing the edges of the frame.*
>
> *The study.*
>
> *The air smells of old paper, candle wax, and something else — something metallic and sharp, like the air before a lightning storm. Whatever Lord Veymar has been doing up here, alone, night after night, the room remembers it.*

**DM Note:** Give the players a moment here. They've been navigating tension and crowds and near-misses for the last twenty minutes. This silence is earned — and unsettling. The contrast between the noisy, golden gala below and this cold, quiet floor should feel wrong. Something is off about the third floor. Something has been off for three months, ever since Veymar brought the box home. Let them feel it before they open the study door and move into Act 5.
